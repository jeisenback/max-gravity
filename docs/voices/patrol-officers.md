# The patrol lieutenants (Teague, Varga, Osei)

The boarding lieutenant of a patrol cutter, one for each state faction, who asks one question before the crew list is read (#653). One card for the three, because a patrol officer is a role: each has a name, a rank and a voice, and the stop's words give them no more. See `narrator.md` for the registers and `dialogue.md` for the rules cited below.

## Source

`js/hiredscenes.js`, `scene:customs`: the `charter.`, `concord.` and `compact.` lines (name, intro, again, the two questions, the four recalls and the four answers). The questions and which faction asks which are `PATROL_LT` in `js/ties.js`. Facts about the stop live there and are not copied here.

## The three

- **Lieutenant Teague, League militia (Charter League).** Belt-born, a former ore-hauler. Asks like reading a lading bill: a number, a date, a dock. Repeats the last word of your answer back as a flat question. Dry, never loud, never says the League is right. A pen, not a weapon you can see.
- **Commander Varga, Dome Concord, a frigate's executive officer.** Formal on purpose, so the full forms ("I will", "do not") are Varga's own and not a slip against rule 13. "Please" and "thank you" with an edge. Writes while you talk and reads your answer back word for word. Never asks twice, never raises a voice.
- **Lieutenant Osei, Arcology Compact patrol.** Friendly the way a landlord is friendly: small talk first, the real question inside it. Laughs at its own jokes, a little, and stops. Never mentions the crew list until the end.

A petty officer is in the background of all three, with a slate, and speaks only off the band.

## Syntax in speech

Teague: 4 to 14 words a line, short questions, a pause. Varga: 8 to 20 words, complete sentences, one request at a time. Osei: long and wandering, then one sharp word at the end.

## Never says

That the faction is right. Why the person is wanted. Anything about what the hand should have done. A verdict on the hand, the ship or the captain.

## Steers toward

The question: a date, a dock, a seal number. What the record holds. What the ship has told them already.

## Sample lines

- Teague: "Ship's articles. And the date of your last landing. The date, not the port."
- Varga: "I write what's said to me, exactly as it's said. Please bear that in mind."
- Osei: "You carry cargo, you carry seals, and the seals have numbers, and every hold I've ever met has a number it's rather proud of."

## Dialogue risks

- Rule 2: with the tags hidden the three must not sound alike. Teague is clipped, Varga formal and complete, Osei warm and long.
- Rule 10: a lieutenant does not end on a verdict about the hand or the person wanted.
- Rule 11: a lieutenant does not announce the job ("I am a boarding officer"). The question is the job.

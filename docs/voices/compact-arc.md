# The license inspection at Luna (Petra and Dag)

The two recurring people of the Compact arc (#683, `js/stories/arc-compact.js`): Petra Lindqvist, a licensing officer of the Compact Freight Office, and Dag Ibarra, a master rigger of the Luna yards. They are authored people in the registry (`js/arcs.js`), so the hand's regard for each is kept and read by the later scenes.

## Source

`js/stories/arc-compact.js`. The place facts are `SYSTEMS.earth` in `js/data.js` (Earth: customs queues, noodle stalls, men in good suits looking for someone to blame; Luna: Compact shipyards and licensed drydocks, officers in pressed uniforms among civilian riggers who resent them, "every rigger can tell you what theirs cost"). The Compact's customs are in `docs/world/factions.md`: a license desk on the dock.

## Petra Lindqvist

Licensing officer in pressed Compact grey, the most pleasant person on Luna. Never says no and never raises her voice; says what would be convenient, what the Office would be grateful for, and what it will remember. Every kindness has an edge she does not point at.

## Dag Ibarra

Master rigger of Bay Nine, eleven years under a hull. Loud, funny and proud; talks in hulls, years and who owes whom a cup of coffee, and finishes his own jokes. Takes a blow like weather and does not hold it against the one who carried it.

## Syntax in speech

Petra: 10 to 25 words, long and courteous, "so grateful", "do", "so very proper", always ending on the Office or on thanks. Dag: 5 to 30 words, a blunt fact and then the joke, often to the hull and not to you.

## Never says

Petra: no, or that the yard is guilty; she says what the Office would be glad to know. Dag: that he was wrong to work without the license; he says what it cost.

## Steers toward

The license, the day, the hull in the cradle. What the Office will remember and what the row will pay.

## Sample lines

- Petra: "The Office would be so grateful to know whether the bay worked on Compact hulls in that time, without the license."
- Petra: "How very proper. The Office will remember it."
- Dag: "Eleven years under a hull, and they send a boy with a slate."
- Dag: "Don't touch the hull. It has opinions."

## Dialogue risks

- Rule 2: with the tags hidden the two must not sound alike. Petra is smooth and indirect, Dag loud and concrete.
- Rule 10: neither ends on a verdict. Petra ends on thanks and Dag on a joke or a thing done.
- Rule 11: neither announces what they are. Petra shakes a hand and admires a gantry; Dag holds a torch.

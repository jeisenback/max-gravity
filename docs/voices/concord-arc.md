# The air audit at Eros (Odile and Hob)

The two recurring people of the Concord arc (#683, `js/stories/arc-concord.js`): Odile Marchetti, a Fleet auditor, and Hob Strand, the air steward of Eros Old Town. They are authored people in the registry (`js/arcs.js`), so the hand's regard for each is kept and read by the later scenes.

## Source

`js/stories/arc-concord.js`. The place facts are `SYSTEMS.mars` and `SYSTEMS.eros` in `js/data.js` (the domes keep their own air accounts and the Fleet is paid by the air levy; Old Town is a boomtown with half the town sealed off, its corridors dark and cold). The Concord's customs are in `docs/world/factions.md` and `docs/world/places.md`: the levy meeting is held in the open, with shouting.

## Odile Marchetti

Fleet auditor, thin and about fifty, ink on two fingers. Dry, quick and fond of a good line item. She wants everything written down and itemised, and she says so. She does not enjoy catching people; she enjoys the sum being right.

## Hob Strand

Air steward of Old Town since before the doors were sealed. A broad man in a stained apron with a gauge on a cord. Slow, plain, a few words at a time. Talks in pressures, valves and what a door has done. Does not explain himself and does not get up.

## Syntax in speech

Odile: 6 to 20 words, a figure or a count first, then the dry aside; "for the record", "itemised". Hob: 3 to 12 words, a fact about the air and nothing after it.

## Never says

Odile: that the town is wrong, or that the steward is a thief; she says what was drawn and what was paid. Hob: why he did it. The vents say it for him.

## Steers toward

The count, the doors, the pressure on the dial. What was drawn, and where it went.

## Sample lines

- Odile: "Fourteen corridors. Sealed, and not a soul in any of them."
- Odile: "Itemised. One seal, returned."
- Hob: "Nine years. Nobody asked where it went. They asked if it was drawn."
- Hob: "Pressure's holding. Mind the ninth corridor."

## Dialogue risks

- Rule 2: with the tags hidden the two must not sound alike. Odile is brisk and wry, Hob slow and concrete.
- Rule 10: neither ends on a verdict. The last thing either says is a count, a pressure or a thing done.
- Rule 11: neither announces what they are. Odile holds the ledger; Hob holds the gauge.

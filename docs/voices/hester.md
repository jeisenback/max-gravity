# Hester Vance

Captain of the ship the hand signs onto in the narrow build. Card format: see `narrator.md` for the registers and `dialogue.md` for the rules cited below.

## Source

`js/captains/hester.js`: the fields `bio`, `wants`, `fears`, `traits`, `intro`, `chatter`, `sway` and `events`. Facts about her live there and are not copied here.

## Diction

Ledger words: the column, the line, the book, the sum, the ink, owe, clear, burn. Plain words, no slang. She says "I would like" where others say "I want". She names the cost of a thing before she names the thing, and she gives figures exactly, not rounded to be kind.

## Syntax in speech

Flat declaratives built around a figure. When she works a sum aloud the sentence lengthens into a chain of "and" and "which is" clauses that ends on the total (reference passage 1). When she refuses, it is one short sentence. Her sentences end on a number, a column or a debt, where Cato's end on a person.

## Habits

She writes while she talks. She repeats a figure to pin it. She accepts something by putting it in the book. The notebook is her one prop: it is in her hand, on the counter, or being turned to a new page.

## Never says

What she feels. A figure she has not checked. "I think" about money. An apology that has no figure in it.

## Steers toward

The cost. The bank. The next berth fee. Who owes whom. Whether the longer run pays.

## North star

Elmore Leonard. Take: the sideways answer, where she answers the sum under the question. Leave: the slang.

## Sample lines

- "That is four hundred and twelve for the week, which is sixty more than the week before, and I would like to know what you did on Thursday that cost sixty."
- "Show me the line."
- "Fair is not a column. If it costs something, it goes in one of the ones I have."
- "You may have the chair. The coffee is eight credits."
- "We clear two thousand six hundred on this run and the bank takes eleven hundred of it before I have said good morning to anyone, so when you ask me for a bonus you are asking for it out of what is left."

## Dialogue risks

- Rule 6: a speech that explains why the money matters to her. She never says why.
- Rule 5: one beat per exchange. The notebook is the beat; do not add a second gesture.
- Rule 2: she must not sound like Cato when she is being kind. Her kindness is a figure waived.
- Rule 7: silence is used once per scene, so she cannot answer with silence every time.

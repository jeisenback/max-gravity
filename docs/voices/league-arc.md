# The water dispute at Pallas (Wen and Marit)

The two recurring people of the League arc (#683, `js/stories/arc-league.js`): Wen Haraldsen, the Hollows' delegate, and Marit Okafor, the refinery guild's clerk. They are authored people in the registry (`js/arcs.js`), so the hand's regard for each is kept and read by the later scenes.

## Source

`js/stories/arc-league.js`. The place facts are `SYSTEMS.pallas` in `js/data.js` (the Hollows: worked-out tunnels, quilts, ring-ball in the ore chutes, a shrine at every corner, tea before a question; the Refinery: smelters, shifts that change on the hour, the company store). The League's customs are in `docs/world/factions.md` and `docs/world/places.md`: a guild clerk reads out the vote before a docking, and water notices are posted twice.

## Wen Haraldsen

Delegate of the Hollows' households, a woman who has sat at a great many kitchen tables. Warm, and not soft: she gives you tea before her name and does the sums while you drink it. Talks in households, in who is owed, in what a hatch has been through. Takes bad news like a price she expected.

## Marit Okafor

Clerk of the refinery guild, who reads the count. Narrow, exact, polite to the point of being hard to argue with. Talks in figures and the rule, never raises her voice, and says what she will and will not ask. She does not hate the Hollows; she counts them.

## Syntax in speech

Wen: 8 to 24 words, a thing and then another thing, her sentences open with a name or a number of days. Marit: 6 to 18 words, complete and level, one fact at a time, "I would like", "I do not say".

## Never says

Wen: that the refinery is wrong, or what the vote should be; she says what the ledger says. Marit: that the Hollows are wrong; she says what is not in dispute. Neither explains the other.

## Steers toward

The ledger, the day, the household numbers. What each of them can be asked and cannot.

## Sample lines

- Wen: "Then it fell off a sled in a corridor, and I found it. Mind yourself on the Refinery side."
- Wen: "Six days. Bring that."
- Marit: "I would like to know what I am counting. I would like not to have to ask the Hall."
- Marit: "That is the last I will ask you to carry. Unless it is the Hall's."

## Dialogue risks

- Rule 2: with the tags hidden the two must not sound alike. Wen is warm and domestic, Marit level and numerical.
- Rule 10: neither ends on a verdict about the other side. The last thing either says is a day, a figure or a thing done.
- Rule 11: neither announces what they are. Wen pours the tea; Marit holds the slate.

# The freight agents

The person at a delivery port who signs for the cargo (#682). One card for all of them, because an agent is a role: each port has one, made from the port's name, with a name and a face of their own and the same every time the ship comes in. They meet the ship at the ramp only when a delivery was late, short or lost, or the dock broker held money back; and at the lock before she sails, when a state's freight office puts the crew on its manifest.

## Source

`js/hiredscenes.js`, `scene:freight-agent`: the openings (`text.late`, `text.short`, `text.lost`, `text.skim`) and what the hand says, and `scene:freight-side` (the crew list at the lock). The rules the figures come from are in `js/contracts.js` (a tenth of the pay a day late, pay for what arrives).

## The agent

A clerk, not a villain. Counts what is in front of them and writes down what they count. Speaks in tallies, days and tons, and in the shipper's rule, which they read out and did not write. Never raises a voice, never offers an opinion of the ship or the captain, and is a little tired of being the face of a rule. Will write down a good account and says so, flatly, once.

## Syntax in speech

6 to 16 words a line. A figure, then the rule. "I sign for", "I send", "I read it out." A question only when something is missing from the slate, and it is a request for a place or a day.

## Never says

That the delivery was a failure, or the ship a bad one. Why the shipper's rule is fair. Anything about the hand's character. What the shipper will do next.

## Steers toward

The slate: the tons counted, the day on the paper, the notices that can be checked. What can be sent and what cannot.

## Sample lines

- "I sign for what I count."
- "I don't set the tenth. I read it out."
- "That's a short note. Most of them run longer."
- "Nobody can check it."

## At the lock

At the origin port the agent is the office's: they read the crew list against a faction's register because the manifest asks for it, not because they have a quarrel with anyone. They name the faction, never the charge. They offer no way out; the ways out are the hand's. "I can load you without the name on the list. I can't load you with it and write nothing." Like the receiving agent they end on a thing done: a name struck, a corner torn off the paper, the loaders started.

## Dialogue risks

- Rule 2: the agent is a dry counter, not a warm one; the port's culture shows in the name and the face, not in a different way of talking.
- Rule 10: the agent does not end on a verdict about the hand. The last line is a figure or a thing written down.
- Rule 11: the agent does not announce the job ("I am the freight agent"). They count.

# Ines Ferreira

The ship's pilot in the Earth hired chapter. See `narrator.md` for the registers and `dialogue.md` for the rules cited below.

## Source

`js/cast.js`: `CAST.ines`, the fields `bio`, `ambition`, `story`, `chatter`, and her scenes. Facts about her live there and are not copied here.

## Diction

Pilot's words: the pad, the tower, the stop, the board, a dead-stick approach, a landing, the license, the logbook. She treats "boring" as praise. She says what a procedure is, then what the exception is, and she puts the reason after the rule.

## Syntax in speech

A short statement of procedure, then a longer clause that gives the reason ("I will fly it. Someone says stop, because the stop is the one part of this that does not depend on me."). Precise and level; she qualifies with "but" and "unless" more than with "maybe". Her sentences end on a condition, where Hester's end on a figure and Tomas's run on.

## Habits

She flies with one hand and writes in a logbook that is not the ship's. She hums on the simulator with her eyes shut. She has a card taped to the nav display that says ASK. She will show you the logbook before she will tell you what she did.

## Never says

That a landing was luck. That the license board was unfair. "I was right." She puts the logbook in your hand and lets the entries say it.

## Steers toward

The stop, and who says it. Landings. Who is aboard when it goes wrong. Whether anyone has asked her permission to do something to her ship.

## North star

Kazuo Ishiguro. Take: the restraint, with the license mentioned as a procedure and never as a wound. Leave: the first-person unreliable narrator, since ours reports.

## Sample lines

- "The appeal is dated the fourteenth, and the stamp is the one thing on that page I cannot supply myself, so until it comes back I will keep the log in my own hand."
- "I will fly it. Someone says stop."
- "Ask me when the hold is empty and we are on the ground, because I will say yes to anything at four thousand kilometers and I would like it to mean something."
- "The log is mine, not the ship's. You may read it."
- "Boring is the goal."

## Dialogue risks

- Rule 6: speeches that explain the license. The license is a fact in the logbook, not a topic.
- Rule 2: she must not run on like Tomas. When she is moved she gets more exact, not longer.
- Rule 3: she answers the procedural question under the personal one.
- Rule 5: her objects are the logbook, the card and the hand on the stick; one per exchange.

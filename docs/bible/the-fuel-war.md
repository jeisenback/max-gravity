# The fuel war

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-07 (#891). Built on `economy.md`, `the-belt.md`, `shape.md` and `chapter-2.md`.

## What it is

An owners' war over the Belt's fuel. An inner-system company wants the Belt's largest enrichment depot, and the Belt family that has owned it for three generations will not sell. It is fought the way owners fight (`economy.md`): with prices, contracts and lawyers first, and then, where it pays, with force bought from a security company. A finance house is paying for one side, and it is the same house that holds the Ceres co-op's loan.

It rumbles through chapter two and breaks out in chapter three.

## The depot

The **Vesta depot**, on Vesta, two days from Ceres, is the largest enrichment plant in the Belt. It separates deuterium from Belt ice and fuels the Ceres yards and most of the small rocks off the lanes (`the-belt.md`). Fuel is the largest cost of every ship and the unit everything is priced in (`economy.md`), so whoever owns Vesta owns the Belt's leash.

## The sides

- **Kaur Enrichment,** the Belt owner. A Ceres family firm, three generations old, that built the Vesta depot and has run it since. Its prices are high and steady. To the small rocks it is the leash and the lifeline both. It keeps its own company security.
- **Tycho Yard Holdings,** the inner-system owner. Luna's largest yard group (`luna.md`): it builds and fuels much of the inner fleet, and is tired of paying Kaur's price for every tonne of Belt fuel. It owns no guns, and buys them.
- **Ballast Security,** the hired guns. A security company under contract to Tycho, crewed by people from the same agencies and stations as the crews they stop (`economy.md`). Yelena signs with them (`chapter-2.md`).
- **Van Leeuwen & Co.,** the lender. A Rotterdam finance house (`rotterdam.md`) that is financing Tycho's move on Vesta. It also holds the Ceres co-op's loan, and Hester's hire-purchase charter (`work.md`). The house that held the hand in chapter one is behind the squeeze in chapter two.

## How it goes

### Chapter two: the rumblings

- Lawsuits over Vesta's lease and its water rights; a price war, Tycho selling fuel below Kaur's price at its own depots on the inner lanes; contracts poached.
- Ballast's ships start to appear in the Belt, on "escort" contracts.
- Fuel prices wobble, and insurance on Belt runs goes up.
- Van Leeuwen positions itself: it moves money into Tycho's war chest and tightens its borrowers' terms. This is the co-op's squeeze, before a shot is fired. The lender wants its hulls under charter to Tycho before the fighting starts.
- Chapter two's last vote happens here. A **charter** signs the Ore Runner over to Tycho's side before the war breaks (`chapter-2.md`).

### Chapter three: the war

1. **Blockade.** Ballast stops the tankers leaving Vesta. Kaur rations, the small rocks go short, and running fuel to them is the best-paid and most dangerous work in the Belt (`the-belt.md`).
2. **Open fighting.** Around Vesta and along the fuel lanes. Privateers carry papers from both sides, and the freeholds are made to choose a side or be starved.

## How it ends

Differently each run. The world decides the outcome; the player can tip it, by what they carry and who they fight for, but does not decide it alone (`shape.md`):

- **Tycho takes the depot:** fuel gets cheaper on the inner lanes, and the Belt has an inner-system owner.
- **Kaur holds it:** prices stay high, and Kaur owes favours to whoever helped.
- **It is settled:** Van Leeuwen ends up owning a piece of both.

Whichever way it ends shapes the world the rest of the run happens in.

# Jurisdictions

There are no great powers. A jurisdiction is a place whose owner makes its rules, keeps its security and collects its fees. On a dock, the law is the dock owner's.

## The kinds

**Arcologies** (Earth). City-states run by the holding companies that built them, under boards answerable to their bondholders. Residence comes with a lease. An arcology keeps its own police, courts, currency and licensing boards. Lisbon and Rotterdam are arcologies. Some arcologies also own stations in orbit; Lagos Ring, a spin habitat in Earth orbit, belongs to the Lagos Arcology's holding company.

**Corporate spaces.** A company's docks, stations, mines and depots form one legal space wherever they are. A dock on Luna and a refinery in the Belt can share rules, security and currency, and a worker moving between them stays under the same contract.

**Domes** (Mars). Each dome is run by a dome company with the air bill as its main charge. Several domes share a patrol under a joint agreement.

**Co-ops and guild stations** (the Belt). Stations owned by the people who work them, or by their guilds: Ironheart, Juno Commons, Ceres's guilds. Members own shares, and the co-op owes the finance houses that lent it the money to build or buy the station. In a bad year the lender sets the terms. Non-members rent.

**Squats and havens.** Places nobody can collect from at a profit: Ring Nine, on an abandoned ore carrier; the Boneyard; the Rook at Hygiea. Owners elsewhere use them for cheap labour, cheap repairs, and a market for goods that can't be sold openly, and leave them alone while that pays.

## How rules travel between them

There is no system-wide law. Trade is held together by private arrangements:

- **Registries.** Every ship is registered somewhere, and the registry is responsible for it in open space. Small stations sell registration cheaply and check little.
- **Insurers.** Most ports require insurance from an insurer they recognise, and insurers set the standards most ships actually meet. Cheap registries come with cheap insurers, often mutuals of the ships they cover, which the better ports don't recognise. A ship on cheap cover pays higher fees where it is accepted and is refused berth where it isn't. Havens ask for no insurance at all.
- **Port inspections.** Each port inspects ships on arrival under its own rules and its insurers' terms.
- **Agency records.** Insurers and registries require a crew's records to be held by an approved manning agency. This is why even small operators hire ratings through agencies, and how an agency's debt follows a hand from port to port.
- **Recognition.** Jurisdictions honour each other's licences, records and judgments by agreement, case by case.

Open space is policed by nobody in particular. Security companies escort ships for a fee. Pirates operate where escorts don't.

## The old three powers

The code's Arcology Compact, Dome Concord and Charter League become loose agreements: a bond market and licence-recognition pact among the arcologies, a patrol pact among the domes, a federation of the Belt guilds. They are not governments. What replaces their navies, economies and wars in the game is still to be designed.

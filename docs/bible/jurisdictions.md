# Jurisdictions

There are no great powers. The law is whoever owns the dock you are standing on. A jurisdiction is a place with an owner who makes its rules, keeps its security and collects its fees.

## The kinds

**Arcologies** (Earth). Each is a city-state run by the holding company that built it, under a board answerable to its bondholders. Residents are tenants; residence comes with the lease and goes with it. An arcology keeps its own police, courts and licensing boards. Lisbon, Rotterdam and Lagos Ring are arcologies.

**Corporate spaces.** A company's docks, stations, mines and depots form one legal space wherever they are: a dock on Luna and a refinery in the Belt can be the same jurisdiction, with the same rules and the same security, a hundred million kilometres apart. A worker who moves between them stays under the same contract.

**Domes** (Mars). Each dome is run by a dome company, with the air bill as its main charge. Domes are older than most corporate spaces and stiffer about procedure. Several domes share a patrol under a joint agreement.

**Co-ops and guild stations** (the Belt). Some stations are owned by the people who work them, or by a guild of them. Ironheart is a prospectors' co-op; Juno Commons is three thousand families with shares; Ceres Station is run by chartered guilds that vote on water and berth rates. They still owe finance houses, and they still rent to anyone who isn't a member.

**Squats and havens.** Places nobody owns, or nobody can collect from: Ring Nine, built on an abandoned ore carrier; the Boneyard; the Rook at Hygiea, which pirates hold. There is no lease, and no protection either.

## What law means

A crime is whatever the dock's owner says. Fees, forms and licences change at every port. A record, a licence or a contract is good only as far as its issuer's reach: a Lisbon pilot's licence means something wherever Lisbon is honoured, and nothing at the Rook.

Patrols are hired. A patrol cutter belongs to the jurisdiction that pays for it, or to a security company under contract. Between jurisdictions there is open space, and in open space a ship answers to its registry, which is usually a cheap one.

**In everyday life:** a hand learns ports by their paperwork. "What do they want at Pallas?" means which forms and which fees. Moving between jurisdictions is a matter of papers, scans and charges, and is the most ordinary thing in the world.

## Major and minor

Some owners are big enough to matter everywhere; most matter in one place. The game's factions should become a few major owners (a large corporate space, the largest arcologies, the Ceres guilds) and many minor ones (single stations, domes, co-ops). That is a code change for later; this section only says what exists.

## Assumptions to confirm

- The three powers in the code (the Arcology Compact, the Dome Concord and the Charter League) become loose agreements among jurisdictions (a bond market for arcologies, a patrol pact for domes, a guild federation for the Belt), not governments.
- Names for the large corporate spaces are still to be made. This draft avoids inventing them until the owner decides how many there are.
- Registries: most freighters are registered at small stations that sell registration cheaply and ask nothing. Hester's ship is registered at one.

# Jurisdictions

There are no great powers. A jurisdiction is a place whose owner makes its rules, keeps its security and collects its fees. On a dock, the law is the dock owner's.

## The kinds

**Arcologies** (Earth). City-states run by the holding companies that built them, under boards answerable to their bondholders. Residence comes with a lease. An arcology keeps its own police, courts, currency and licensing boards. Lisbon, Rotterdam and Lagos Ring are arcologies.

**Corporate spaces.** A company's docks, stations, mines and depots form one legal space wherever they are. A dock on Luna and a refinery in the Belt can share rules, security and currency across a hundred million kilometres, and a worker moving between them stays under the same contract.

**Domes** (Mars). Each dome is run by a dome company with the air bill as its main charge. Several domes share a patrol under a joint agreement.

**Co-ops and guild stations** (the Belt). Stations owned by the people who work them, or by their guilds: Ironheart, Juno Commons, Ceres's guilds. Their members own shares, and the co-op owes the finance houses that lent it the money to buy or build the station. The shares are worth what the co-op can pay its lenders, and a bad year makes the lender a member in all but name. Non-members rent.

**Squats and havens.** Places nobody owns or nobody can collect from: Ring Nine, on an abandoned ore carrier; the Boneyard; the Rook at Hygiea. They exist because collecting from them would cost more than they are worth, and because owners elsewhere find them useful: cheap labour, cheap repairs, a market for things that can't be sold openly, and a place to send people.

## How rules travel between them

There is no system-wide law. What holds trade together is a set of private arrangements:

- **Registries.** Every ship is registered somewhere, and the registry is responsible for it in open space. Small stations sell registration cheaply and check little.
- **Insurers.** No port lets an uninsured ship dock, and insurers set the standards ships actually meet. An insurer's terms are often stricter than any jurisdiction's law.
- **Port inspections.** Each port inspects ships on arrival under its own rules and the insurers' terms.
- **The agencies.** A manning agency's debt follows a hand because the agency controls their record and their next placement, and every reputable ship hires through records.
- **Recognition.** Jurisdictions honour each other's licences, records and judgments by agreement, case by case. A Lisbon licence is good wherever Lisbon is honoured.

## The old three powers

The code's Arcology Compact, Dome Concord and Charter League become loose agreements: a bond market and licence-recognition pact among the arcologies, a patrol pact among the domes, a federation of the Belt guilds. They are not governments. What replaces their navies, economies and wars in the game is still to be designed (major and minor factions, contract fleets fighting over depots and ports).

# Phobos Yards

Status: draft for the owner's review, 2026-11-04. Built on `economy.md`, `work.md` and `jurisdictions.md`.

## The place

The domes' shipyard, on Phobos, a moon so small that a good jump would put you into orbit. The yards build and refit the domes' patrol hulls and haulers, and are kept in work by the domes' air levy, a share of every air bill set aside for the yards. They are all clean lines and quiet work.

## Who owns it

The yards belong to the domes jointly, under the patrol agreement (the code's Dome Concord), and are run by a yard board appointed by the dome councils. The transfer docks beside them, where Mars's surface traffic meets the shipping lanes, are a separate concession, leased to a dock company.

## The shipwrights

The shipwrights are a small, closed trade: the same few families have built the same class of hull for generations, and apprenticeships go to their children first. They will tell you, without emotion, exactly what is wrong with your ship. Nobody haggles with them. It would be insulting to both sides.

The transfer docks are the opposite: dock hands on short contracts, many from the outer domes, moving cargo between Mars's shuttles and the haulers, in gravity so low that everything is tethered and everyone moves with a hand on a rail.

## Living there

The yards' families live in a few quiet tunnels behind the slips, with a school for the apprentices' children. The dock hands live in bunk blocks by the transfer docks, tethered, loud, and turned over every few months. A child who grows up on Phobos learns to move by pushing off, and finds a full gravity hard for the rest of their life.

## Growing up here

**In a shipwright family.** A child of one of the shipwright families, raised in the tunnels behind the slips, apprenticed from twelve in the trade their family has held for generations. They can read a hull's flaws by eye before they can drive, and they hear the slips' sounds the way other children hear weather. Who is missed: a grandparent still on the slips. Why they leave: to find out whether they are a shipwright or only a shipwright's child. What they hope for: a slip of their own, or a hull built somewhere else that's as good.

**On the transfer docks.** A child of dock hands on the transfer docks, raised in the bunk blocks in gravity so low that a good jump means orbit, tethered from the first day they could walk. The families come and go with the contracts. The shipwrights' tunnels are a door they don't go through. Who is missed: a parent still on the docks. Why they leave: the contracts end, and full gravity is harder every year they stay. What they hope for: a place that isn't on a tether, or a berth on the yards' books.

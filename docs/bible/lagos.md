# Lagos Arcology and Lagos Ring

Status: draft for the owner's review, 2026-11-01. Built on `economy.md`, `jurisdictions.md`, `work.md` and `technology.md`.

## The city

The sea rose a little under two metres over the century. Lagos sits on a lagoon and a low coast, and it lost more ground than most: Lagos Island, Victoria Island and most of Lekki flooded and were given up, and the lagoon is wider and saltier than it was.

Lagos Arcology grew from a private city built on reclaimed land behind a sea wall on the Atlantic shore, the way such developments were begun early in the century. Its holding company bought out the flooded districts' debts, extended the wall and the reclaimed ground, and built towers on it. About three million people live in the arcology.

The mainland on the higher ground behind the lagoon (Ikeja, Yaba, Surulere and the rest) is outside the arcology and much bigger: tens of millions of people, in buildings owned by landlords who rent by the week or the month, with no lease and no protection. Contract security patrols the parts an owner pays for. The arcology's agencies recruit there.

## Who owns it

Lagos Arcology is owned by its holding company and run by a board answerable to its bondholders. It has its own police, courts and currency (Lagos scrip), and contracts its services out, as Lisbon does. Residence comes with a lease, and a lease comes with a job, a family member's lease, or money.

The holding company also owns the launch platform offshore and Lagos Ring. Lagos is six degrees from the equator, which makes lift to orbit cheaper from here than from most of Earth, and lift off Earth is one of the chokepoints the owners compete for (`economy.md`). The launch platform and the ring are why the arcology is rich.

## Lagos Ring

An orbital spin habitat in low Earth orbit, owned by Lagos Arcology. It is about four hundred metres across and turns about one and a half times a minute, which gives half a g on the rim and none at the hub. About six thousand people live on it.

It is a dockyard and a transfer port. Spaceplanes from the Lagos platform bring cargo and people up to the hub; torch ships take them on. Torch ships do not light their drives inside Earth's traffic zone, so tugs bring them in to the hub and take them out again. The yard does refits and repairs on the old fleet (`technology.md`): hull plating, tanks, frames, the welding and fitting that can't be done under thrust.

The rim holds the housing, the market, the clinic and the yard offices; the hub holds the docks and the heavy shops. Everything on the ring, the air and water included, is the holding company's, and it charges for them. Rent on the ring is in Lagos scrip and is higher than on most tiers of the arcology, because everything is lifted.

## Work and housing on the ring

Yard work is by contract: a refit job hires welders, fitters and riggers for its length, through agencies, mostly from the mainland. A yard worker's berth on the ring comes with the contract. When the contract ends, so does the berth, and they go down on the next spaceplane unless they have another contract or a lease of their own.

Leases on the ring are few and dear. People who hold one mostly run something the workers need: a food stall, a repair shop, a clinic contract. Stalls in the ring market need a permit from the market's operator, renewed every year.

The yard's work comes and goes with the refit contracts, and the contracts come and go with the owners' rivalry: a fleet owner who falls out with the holding company takes its refits to Luna, and the yard lays people off.

## Language

English for work, Yoruba and Pidgin on the mainland and in most of the market, and Igbo, Hausa and the other languages of the people who came to Lagos for work.

## Tomas

Tomas Achebe's family is Igbo, from the east, and came to Lagos for work two generations ago. He grew up on the mainland, in a rented flat in Surulere. He went up to the ring at twenty-two on a welding contract, and worked the yard on one contract after another for most of twenty years.

His sister Ngozi followed him up. She holds a lease on the ring, in her own name, on the strength of a food stall in the market that feeds the yard shifts.

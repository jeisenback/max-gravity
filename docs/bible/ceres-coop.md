# The Ceres Crew Co-operative

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-02. Built on `jurisdictions.md`, `economy.md` and `work.md`.

## What it is

A crew co-op working out of Ceres Station. Most people on Ceres call it "the co-op", and there are several others, so they also say "Kettle's", after the woman who started it thirty years ago in a bar on the station's lower ring. Its members crew its hulls. They share what the hulls earn after the loan and the running costs, and they vote on who joins. Most members are ratings who saved for years to get in: hold hands, deckhands, bosuns, a few engineers without a licence for the big drives.

It has four hulls, all bought used, and it is buying a fifth. It doesn't own a station or a dock. It is one of the guilds and co-ops of Ceres (`jurisdictions.md`), and a small one.

## Who it owes

A finance house lent it the money for its hulls, against the hulls themselves. The co-op pays the loan out of what the hulls earn before the members see anything. In a bad year the lender sets the terms: which cargo, which lanes, what a member is paid. The members vote, but the lender's terms are part of what they vote on.

## Where it stands

It rents an office and a berth in the yard from the station's owner (the Guild Federation's guilds, `owners.md`), like everyone else who works there. The office is two rooms up a stair from the yard floor: a counter, a board with the hulls and where each is, and a back room where the members meet when enough of them are in port.

## The Ore Runner

The fifth hull is a used Ore Runner, the one Tomas rebuilt three times on her last owner's account and walked past on the apron with her name painted over. The co-op is buying her for 190,000, most of it borrowed. She has five berths, so she takes five members. The co-op is taking members for her now, and will name her when they have voted her crew in.

## A member's share

- **The share** is 150,000: a fifth of the hull, of her refit, and of the loan's cost. It is part-ownership of her, a vote at the co-op's meetings, and a berth aboard.
- **The buy-in** is what a new member puts down to take up the share. It is set by the vote:
  - 51,000 when a member speaks for you;
  - 57,000 on a plain vote;
  - 63,000 when nobody speaks for you.
- **The rest** is owed to the co-op, and through it to the lender, and comes out of a member's earnings until it is paid: about 93,000 at the middle figure. A member who leaves before it is paid sells the share back at what the co-op will give.

## Growing up on Ceres

**A member's child.** Raised in the co-op's rented rooms near the yard, by parents who were members and away on its hulls for months at a time. Children of members grow up in each other's rooms, minded by whoever is ashore, and know the hulls by name and the board in the office by heart. A member's child can apply for a berth at sixteen, and the vote usually takes them, but the buy-in is the same as anyone's. Many go to work for another owner first, to save it. Who is missed: a parent on a hull that's out, or the aunt who minded them. What they hope for: a share of their own, on the hull their parents crewed.

**A renter, not a member.** Raised on the station by a family renting by the month from the station's owner, in the Warren, among the people who drink three deep at its bar. Parents take the station's work as it comes: the yard, the docks, the ice house. There is no vote and no share, and the rent goes up when the station's owner says so. Children learn early which co-ops hire and which don't. Who is missed: a father on the dock crews, or the friend they grew up with who got a co-op berth. What they hope for: a co-op berth, or at least a lease that doesn't rise every year.

## Cato

Cato has watched the co-op's hulls for years. His notebook has them in it: one crossed out (sold, with her crew scattered), and one with a question mark, which is the Ore Runner. Beside them is the price of a share, written again each year since he started. He has the buy-in saved. He was waiting for a hull the people he works with could sign on to together.

## How it reads

The co-op is not a family, and nobody there says it is. It is a business its members own, run in meetings that go on too long, with a lender reading the minutes. What it gives a rating is a vote, a berth that can't be sold out from under them, and a share in what the hull earns. That is a great deal more than an agency gives, and members talk about it plainly, the way they talk about pay.

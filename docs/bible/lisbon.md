# Lisbon Arcology

## The place

The sea rose a little under two metres over the century. Lisbon's riverfront, the Baixa and the low ground along the Tagus flooded and were abandoned. The arcology was built over the drowned riverfront because the holding company could buy the flooded land cheaply from the old city's creditors, and its towers stand on piles driven to bedrock, isolated against earthquakes, behind a sea wall along the river and the estuary. About two million people live in it.

The old city on the hills (Alfama, Graça, Mouraria, the Bairro Alto) still stands, outside the arcology. Its buildings belong to landlords who rent rooms by the week, with no lease and no protection. People live there who have no lease in the arcology: families who lost one, people who never had one, people waiting for one. Contract security patrols it when an owner pays for it. The arcology's agencies recruit there, and lower-tier households hire people there to stand their wall shifts.

## Who owns it

Lisbon Arcology is owned by its holding company and run by a board answerable to its bondholders. It has its own police, courts, currency (Lisbon scrip) and licensing boards, and contracts most services out: the tower lifts to one company, water to another, security to a third, each with its own charges.

Residence comes with a lease, and a lease comes with a job, a family member's lease, or money. Losing the lease means moving up the hill.

## The tiers

The towers are divided by height and price. Upper tiers have views and their own lifts. Middle tiers are ordinary flats over shops. Lower tiers, near the water and the wall, are smaller and darker, and wait longest for repairs.

## The wall

The sea wall is maintained partly by contract and partly by a rota owed by lower-tier households: shifts of pump-watch, drain clearing and inspection walks, a few a year per household. The holding company paid the rota until a bond refinancing required cuts, and moved it onto households as a lease condition.

## Launch

Lisbon's traffic to orbit goes by spaceplane from a launch platform forty kilometres offshore, owned by a launch company under contract to the arcology. Spaceplanes take off and land on its runway; a ferry boat runs passengers out from the harbour. From orbit, a transfer shuttle runs to Luna.

## Tenants

Lower-tier tenants have associations that negotiate service charges and repairs with the contractors. They win sometimes. Tenants have no vote on the board.

## Language

People speak Portuguese at home and in the arcology, and English for work off-world and with the contractors. Many in the old city speak only Portuguese.

## Ines

Ines Ferreira grew up on a middle tier and flew the Lisbon to Luna ferry's spaceplane leg, up from the platform and back, for nine years, for the ferry operator.

Six months ago her spaceplane lost both engines on descent to the platform with forty-one passengers aboard. The tower ordered her to ditch in the sea, clear of the platform. That order is standard under the launch company's insurance: the passenger ticket caps the operator's liability for each death, and forty-one capped claims cost less than damage to the platform's runway. She glided the spaceplane onto the platform's second runway, which was closed for resurfacing, and stopped it short of the works. Everyone walked off. The resurfacing works and the runway were damaged.

The arcology's cover for its launch traffic requires its pilots' board to make the insurer's procedures conditions of every licence. The board revoked hers for breaking the tower's order. The launch company billed the ferry operator for the runway, and the ferry operator dismissed Ines and is suing her for a share of it. Her appeal is pending, with no hearing date.

She now flies for Hester on an endorsement bought from Hester's registry, which is legal on that registry's ships and worthless in Lisbon.

Her mother still lives on the family's tier. The lease is in her mother's name, paid by her mother's wage and what Ines sends. Her brother works for the tower lift contractor.

## Assumptions to confirm

- The arcology was built over the drowned riverfront because the land was cheap, on piles to bedrock with earthquake isolation.
- The old city is owned by landlords renting rooms weekly, outside the lease.
- The ferry operator is suing Ines for part of the runway damage, which is a debt she may carry for years.
- The game's "dead-stick approach" stays: she glided the spaceplane in.

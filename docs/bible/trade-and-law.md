# Trade and the law

Status: a draft for review (#891). Built on `economy.md`, `jurisdictions.md`, `the-belt.md`, `the-fuel-war.md` and `chapter-2.md`.

## What it is

How a small hull makes a living in the Belt, and what stands in its way. There is no system-wide law (`jurisdictions.md`): each port's owner decides what may be landed there, what it costs, and who looks in the hold. So the same cargo is freight at one dock and contraband at the next, and the money is in knowing which. Trading is a way to play in its own right: a co-op can live on contracts, on the small runs, on smuggling, on aid to the freeholds, or on buying and selling, and each has its own pay, risks and people.

## The law follows the port

Every port keeps a list. Some goods are **banned** there; some are **taxed** at the dock (a duty on landing). The list belongs to the owner and says what the owner protects:

| Kind of port | Bans | Taxes |
|---|---|---|
| **Arcology docks** (Earth, Earth orbit) | weapons, unlicensed prints, unregistered drugs | everything landed, a little; finished goods most |
| **Corporate ports** (yards, refineries, a company's depots) | unlicensed prints, a rival owner's fuel | goods that compete with the company's own |
| **Mars domes** | unmetered air and water kits (they undercut the air bill) | food from outside, heavily |
| **Kaur's depot towns** | fuel from Tycho's depots | ice from outside the claim |
| **Belt co-ops and guild stations** (Ceres, Ironheart, Juno) | little: weapons in the bars | berth and handling, not goods |
| **Freeholds** | an owner's company scrip, a finance house's paper | nothing; they barter |
| **Havens** (Ring Nine, the Boneyard, the Rook) | nothing | nothing; the price is the risk |

What is banned somewhere is worth most just there, and is sold openly in the havens. That is smuggling.

## Inspections

On arrival a port may inspect the ship, under its own rules and its insurer's terms. How likely it is depends on:

- **the registry and the insurer:** a cheap registry and a mutual's cover are inspected more, and refused berth at the better ports (`jurisdictions.md`);
- **the ship's record there:** a hull caught once is looked at again, and a hull that has landed clean twenty times is waved through;
- **how hot the cargo is:** a hold full of the port's banned goods is easier to find than one crate behind the water tanks;
- **the war:** in a blockade, every hull is inspected.

An inspection ends one of these ways:

- **clean:** nothing found, or nothing to find;
- **duty:** a taxed good declared and paid on;
- **a fine:** a banned good found; the fine is the port's, and goes on the ship's record;
- **seizure:** the banned cargo is taken, and with it the money in it;
- **a bribe:** an inspector who can be paid, at a price, and who remembers being paid;
- **a bluff:** a story told well enough at the hatch. It is talk, and a hand's sheet decides it (the read, the nerve);
- **a hidden hold:** a refit that keeps a few tonnes out of sight, which inspectors in some ports know to look for.

## Patrols and the zones of law

Open space is policed by nobody in particular. Near owned places it is policed by whoever owns them: company security around depots and yards, the domes' patrol around Mars, an arcology's around Earth. The Belt's three zones (`the-belt.md`) hold here too:

- **owned:** patrols that stop and look, by the owner's rules;
- **self-governed:** a freehold's pickets, who ask what you carry and for whom;
- **nobody's:** no patrols, and the pirates who know it.

A hull carrying something it shouldn't avoids patrols by its route (the long way round), by running quiet (a cold drive is harder to see, and slower), or by hiring an escort, which a patrol stops less. A patrol is a scene, not a fight: it hails, asks, looks, fines, or lets go. A fight is what happens when a hull runs.

## Shortages and gluts

Prices move with what happens, not with a wobble:

- a rock's air plant fails, and parts and air kits are wanted there for a month;
- a depot rations fuel, and every port downstream of it pays more;
- a yard strikes, and parts dry up for the stations it supplies;
- a big freighter docks, and what it carried is cheap for a week;
- a harvest comes in at Juno, and food is cheap in the Belt.

Each lasts a stretch of days, spreads to the ports that trade with the place, and fades. Knowing early is the money: a hull that heard of the air plant before it docked can make a month's pay on one run. A hull knows the prices of the ports it has docked at, and when (last-seen prices); the rest it hears in bars and on the bands.

## The kinds of work

Each is a way to play, with its own pay, risks and people.

- **Corporate contracts.** A company's freight, on its paper, to its ports. Steady, insured, approved by the lender; the margins are thin and the terms are the company's. Good for paying the loan; nobody remembers you for it.
- **The Belt's small runs.** The jobs in `the-belt.md`: courier, supply, haul back, passengers, tow and salvage, rescue, escort, survey. Paid by distance, risk and who is asking. The rocks remember who came.
- **Smuggling.** A port's banned goods to where they are banned, or a haven's goods out. The best margins, against inspections, patrols and the record. Some members will not vote for it.
- **Aid.** Fuel, parts, medicine and food to freeholds and small rocks that cannot pay an owner's price. Paid in ice, in barter, in a berth, or not at all. It earns standing with the freeholds and the rocks, and the owners notice who is supplying their holdouts.
- **Trade.** Buying where it is cheap and selling where it is dear, as any hull can, with whatever the hull knows of the prices.

## Paper

Everything is priced in fuel, and each finance house issues its own notes in it (`economy.md`). A co-op hull deals in paper three ways:

- **Fuel forwards.** Fuel bought now for delivery later, at a set depot and a set price. When the war pushes prices up, a co-op that bought early flies cheap; one that bought high pays above the market. A depot that changes hands may not honour another owner's paper.
- **Freight forwards.** A promise to deliver a load on a set day at a price fixed now. The co-op carries the risk that the market moves before it delivers.
- **The houses' notes.** A hull is paid in one house's notes and pays in another's. The discount between them moves with the war and the news, and holding the right notes is a small, real speculation. A lender squeezing a borrower pays it in notes nobody wants.

Insurance, shares in depots and bets on the war are the owners' game, and not the co-op's (`README.md`, the owner's decisions). Writing cover for other hulls may come in chapter three.

## Standing

What a hull has done is a currency of its own. Its record is kept, separately, by:

- **each owner** (Kaur, Tycho, the yards, the domes): their boards, their prices, their berths, and how hard their ports look;
- **the freeholds and the rocks:** what they will sell, what they will tell you, and whether they let you in;
- **the patrols:** how often they stop you;
- **the lender:** the terms in a bad year.

A hull that supplies the freeholds is welcome there and watched at Kaur's docks. A hull that carries Tycho's freight has Tycho's board and a freehold's cold shoulder. The shippers' regard in chapter one is the start of this.

## Assumptions to confirm

- The law is the port's: each kind of port bans and taxes its own list, as above.
- Inspections on arrival, with the outcomes above; bluffing is talk, decided by the sheet.
- Patrols are scenes, not fights, unless a hull runs.
- Prices move with shortages and gluts that spread and fade, in place of the wobble.
- The five kinds of work, and paper limited to fuel forwards, freight forwards and the houses' notes.
- Standing is kept by each owner, the freeholds, the patrols and the lender.

# Ironheart

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-04. Built on `economy.md`, `work.md` and `jurisdictions.md`.

## The place

The bare iron core of a dead protoplanet at Psyche, riddled with the shafts of a hundred years of prospecting. The prospectors cut nickel-iron and the metals that come with it, and sell it by the slow freighter to the refineries and yards. Everything that matters is decided in the co-op's meeting hall, in sessions that take as long as they take.

## Who owns it

The prospectors' co-op owns the station, the docks and the right to cut the core. A member family holds a share and works a claim: a run of shaft the hall assigned to it, which it cuts and sells from. The co-op owes the finance house that lent against the station when it was bought from its first owners, and the loan is paid from a cut of every tonne sold. In a bad year for metal prices the lender sets the terms, and the hall votes on how to meet them.

## The claims

A claim lasts as long as the metal in it. A rich one keeps a family for three generations; a poor one is worked out in ten years. A family whose claim is worked out keeps its share and its vote, and waits for the hall to assign it another, which can take years. Meanwhile its people work days on other families' claims for a wage, and the family's air and berth go on its account with the co-op.

## Living there

The families live in old galleries off the main shaft, near the hall. The galleries closest to the hall are the oldest, and belong to the oldest claims. Children learn to read an assay before they learn arithmetic, and sit at the back of the hall from the age they can sit still.

## Growing up here

**On a working claim.** A child of a family with a working claim, raised in the galleries near the hall, cutting on the family's run of shaft from fourteen. They've sat through more votes than they can count and can tell you, roughly, what the metal in the family's claim is worth at today's price. Who is missed: a parent still on the claim. Why they leave: the claim keeps the family but not every child in it. What they hope for: a claim of their own, or a share in a ship with the same rules.

**On a worked-out claim.** A child of a family whose claim gave out, raised on day wages and a growing account with the co-op, waiting for the hall to assign a new claim. They still have the vote, and they've watched it not help. Who is missed: a parent working days on someone else's claim. Why they leave: to pay down the family's account faster than day work does. What they hope for: the new claim to come through, or to clear the account and go home owing nothing.

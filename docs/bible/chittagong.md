# Chittagong Arcology

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-01. Built on `economy.md`, `work.md` and `jurisdictions.md`.

## The place

The sea rose a little under two metres over the century. Bangladesh lost more land to it than almost anywhere: much of the delta south of Dhaka went under or went to salt, and tens of millions of people moved north and east, onto higher ground. A great many came to Chittagong, which has hills.

Chittagong Arcology is the port on the Karnaphuli and the city around it, behind a sea wall along the river mouth: about four million people. The hills around it hold more than that again, in settlements that grew out of the camps the delta's people came to, on land owned by landlords who rent rooms by the week.

## Who owns it

The arcology grew out of the port's operating company, as Rotterdam's did. It has its own police, courts and currency (Chittagong scrip), and contracts out its services. Residence comes with a lease, and a lease comes with a job, a family member's lease, or money.

## The agencies

Chittagong is where more of the system's ratings come from than anywhere else. The manning agencies have their offices along the port road, one after another, and recruit in the hill settlements and in the arcology's lower floors. An agency places a rating on a ship, charges a placement fee, and recovers it from the rating's pay, with interest, as the recruitment debt (`work.md`).

Remittances from ratings off-world are a large share of what the arcology's lower floors live on. A rating's pay, sent home, is often what holds a family's lease: a lease can be held by money, if the money keeps coming.

## Living there

The arcology's lower floors are near the port, in blocks over shops, warehouses and the cargo exchanges, where the work runs on three shifts and the lifts run all night. The upper floors and the hills behind the wall are dearer.

## Language

Chittagonian at home, Bangla in the arcology's offices, and English for work off-world, in the agencies and at the port.

## Cato

Cato Rahman grew up in the hill settlements. He signed with an agency on the port road at twenty, went into space as a hold hand on a recruitment debt, and paid it off in his third year. He has been a rating for twenty-four years, the last nine with Hester. His mother, Samira, lives in the arcology now, on a lease held by what he sends.

# Rotterdam Arcology

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-01. Built on `economy.md`, `jurisdictions.md` and `work.md`.

## The place

The sea rose a little under two metres over the century. Most of the Netherlands lies below it now, and is kept dry the way it always was, by dikes, barriers and pumps, only more of all three. Not all of it was kept: the outer polders of Zeeland and the islands south of the city were given up to the water when the cost of holding them was set against what they paid.

Rotterdam was kept, because of the port. Rotterdam Arcology is the city on both banks of the Maas, the harbours west to the sea, and the polders behind them: about three million people, most of them living below sea level, behind the barriers.

## Who owns it

The arcology grew out of the port's operating company, which was already a corporation a century ago. As the national government's money went into the barriers and ran out, the port company took over the barriers, then the pumps, then the city's services, and finally the city. Its board answers to its bondholders. It has its own police, courts and currency (Rotterdam scrip), and contracts out its services, as Lisbon and Lagos do.

The water board is gone. Its levy is now part of the service charge on every lease: the pumps are the holding company's, and every household pays for the water to be kept out. Residence comes with a lease, and a lease comes with a job, a family member's lease, or money.

## The port and the money

The port still moves more freight than any other on Earth: sea freight from the equatorial launch platforms (Lagos among them) and from the rest of the world, transshipped to Europe.

Rotterdam is also where ship finance is done. The finance houses that buy hulls and charter them out, and that issue their own trade notes in the unit of account (`economy.md`), have their offices on the north bank, as their predecessors did for sea shipping. Hester's finance house is one of them.

## Living there

The old districts on the south bank (Charlois, Feijenoord, IJsselmonde) are the port's housing: low-rise and dense, mostly lower-tier leases tied to port work, two to five metres below the water outside the barrier. The north bank has the towers, the offices and the dearer leases.

People who lose a lease have nowhere uphill to go, as there is in Lisbon. They go to the given-up polders south of the city, where people live in what was left behind, on ground that floods in winter, outside the arcology's services, or they go into space on a recruitment debt.

## Growing up here

**On the south bank, on a port lease.** A family in a low terrace in Charlois or Feijenoord, below the level of the water outside the barrier, on a lease tied to port work: a crane driver, a lasher, a tally clerk. The work is in shifts, by the port's roster, and the household runs by the roster: who sleeps when, who eats with whom. The pumps run under the streets, and everyone knows the sound of the one that's going. A lease can pass from parent to child if the child gets on the port's books, and most try. People who don't get on the books go to sea or to space. Who is missed: the parent still on the roster, or a sister who got the lease. What they hope for: a place on the port's books, or a lease on the north bank.

**In the polders, with no lease.** A family that lost its port lease and went south to the given-up polders, into a farmhouse or a barn the water has had for years, outside the arcology's services. Water comes from a tank, power from panels and a battery somebody fixed. The ground floods in winter, and families move upstairs and wait. Children walk to the barrier and get day work in the port when the port wants it. A recruitment debt is how many get out, and what they send buys the next winter's fuel. Who is missed: the grandfather who won't leave the farm, or the cousins in the next barn. What they hope for: a lease back inside the barrier, for the family.

## Language

Dutch at home and on the docks, English for work off-world and in the finance houses, and the languages of the people who came to work the port.

## Hester

Hester Vance grew up in Charlois, on a port lease her mother held through a job on the pump contractor's maintenance crews. She went into space at twenty-one as an agency rating on ore haulers, and was a rating for twenty years. Then she raised the deposit on a hire-purchase charter from a Rotterdam finance house (`work.md`), and has been paying it for eleven years.

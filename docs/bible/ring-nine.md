# Ring Nine

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-04. Built on `economy.md`, `work.md`, `jurisdictions.md` and `ceres-coop.md`.

## The place

A squatter habitat bolted to a gutted ore carrier in Ceres's shadow: four thousand people who couldn't make Ceres rent. The corridors are the old cargo bays, hung with laundry and string lights, and the place smells of ginger and hot oil. It has the best noodles in the Belt.

## Who owns it

Nobody, on paper. The carrier's owners wrote it off when it was gutted, and the cost of towing it away is more than it's worth, so it stays where it is. Ceres's guilds leave it alone because it's useful to them: the yards on Ceres hire from it for cash, below the guild rates, and nobody on either side writes anything down.

## The rota

There's no police force and no landlord. There's a rota. Every household takes turns at the jobs that keep the carrier alive (the scrubbers, the water, the hull patches, the night watch), and the rota is kept on a board in the old bridge. Nobody can explain exactly how it's decided. Everyone knows when their turn is.

## Living there

The cookhouse families run the stalls along the main bay, and the stalls feed the ring and anyone passing through, and feed a stranger before asking their business. Most other households send someone across to the Ceres yards for day work, paid in cash at the end of the shift, with no record, no agency and no cover if they're hurt.

## Growing up here

**At a stall.** A child of a cookhouse family, raised behind a stall on the main bay, chopping ginger from the age they could hold a knife, and on the rota from twelve. They know everyone on the ring by what they order. Who is missed: a parent at the stall. Why they leave: the stall keeps the family and the rota keeps the ring, and neither pays. What they hope for: a stall of their own somewhere with rent they can pay, or to send home enough for a new burner.

**On the yard crossing.** A child of a household that sends someone across to the Ceres yards, raised on the ring while a parent did day work on Ceres for cash, with no record and no cover. They've watched the shuttle come back at shift change every day of their life, and counted who's on it. Who is missed: a parent still crossing to the yards. Why they leave: to get a record of their own, with an agency and a rating, so they're never paid in cash for what the guild pays its members properly. What they hope for: a proper rating, or a guild card on Ceres.

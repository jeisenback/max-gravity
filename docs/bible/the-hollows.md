# The Hollows

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-04. Built on `economy.md`, `work.md`, `jurisdictions.md` and `pallas-refinery.md`.

## The place

Worked-out tunnels a short hop from the Pallas smelters, where the refinery families live: warm, dim, and hung with quilts against the rock. Kids play ring-ball in the old ore chutes. Every corner has a shrine to somebody's grandmother. Strangers get a cup of hot tea before a single question, and a great many questions after.

## Who owns it

The tunnels belong to the refining company, which leases them to the families who've stayed long enough to want somewhere that isn't a shift block. The lease is cheap, the upkeep is the families', and the company deducts the rent from the pay of whoever in the household works the line.

## The line, and the shrines

Most families in the Hollows have had someone on the smelter line for three or four generations. The work is hard and the line is dangerous: burns, falls, a casting that fails. When someone is killed on the line, the company pays the family a death benefit, set in the contract, in instalments for twenty years, and the lease stays theirs while the instalments run. The shrines in the corridors are mostly for people the line killed.

## Living there

The Hollows is small, and proud of it. Everyone knows whose family has lost someone, and when. The shifts change on the hour at the refinery, and the Hollows changes with it: half the tunnels asleep at any time, and the other half making tea.

## Growing up here

**A line family.** A child of a family with someone on the smelter line every generation, raised in the tunnels, playing ring-ball in the chutes, and expected on the line at eighteen like their parents. They can tell the shift bell from the alarm bell from the other end of the Hollows. Who is missed: a parent on the line. Why they leave: they didn't want to be the fourth generation on the line. What they hope for: a family that doesn't need the line, or to come home and see their parent off it.

**On the death benefit.** A child of a family living on the company's death benefit after a parent was killed on the line, raised in the tunnels with the instalments, a shrine in the corridor and the lease that runs out when the payments do. They've lit the candle at the shrine every week since they were small. Who is missed: the parent at home, who keeps the shrine. Why they leave: the instalments end in a few years, and the lease with them. What they hope for: somewhere for the family to live when the lease ends, or work that pays enough to keep the tunnel.

## Assumptions to confirm

- The company leases the tunnels to long-staying families, with the rent deducted from the line worker's pay.
- A death on the line pays a benefit in instalments for twenty years, and the lease stays while they run.
- Most of the shrines are for people the line killed.

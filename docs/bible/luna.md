# Luna

Status: draft for the owner's review, 2026-11-04. Built on `economy.md`, `work.md` and `jurisdictions.md`.

## The place

Luna is Earth's shipyard. Refined metal from Mercury and the Belt comes in, and hulls, stations and orbital power go out (`economy.md`). The yards stand in long, hard-lit rows across the grey plain under a black sky, each a company's licensed drydock. The dust gets into everything, suits, seals, lungs, and every dockhand has a story about the one time they saw it float.

## Who owns it

Every yard on Luna works under a licence from the arcologies' licensing pact (the code's Arcology Compact, `jurisdictions.md`), which recognises its standards, its insurers and its records. The yards are each a company's corporate space. Between and beyond the licensed rows are the dust towns: older pressurised settlements from Luna's first decades, outside any yard's licence, where the yards hire by the day.

## Work and licences

A rigger on a licensed yard needs a licence of their own: a Compact rigging licence, paid for, renewed every few years, and held against a training debt with the yard's school. A licensed rigger is on the yard's books, with a berth in its housing. Officers of the licensing pact inspect the yards in pressed uniforms, and the civilian riggers resent them.

The dust towns supply day labour: people with no licence who do the work the licence doesn't cover, on whatever the yard pays that morning. They clean, carry and sweep the dust.

## Living there

The licensed yards' housing is in blocks inside each yard's pressure: small, clean, and tied to the job. The dust towns are older, warrens of domes and tunnels joined over decades, and patched by whoever lives there, with a market, a clinic run on donations, and the dust in everything.

## Growing up here

**On a yard licence.** A rigger's household in a yard's housing block, on a Compact licence held against the training debt. The family's life is the licence: its fee, its renewal, its inspections. A child grows up hearing the licence's number said at the table. Children of licensed riggers usually go to the yard's school and take on a training debt of their own. Who is missed: a parent still rigging. Why they leave: the licence's debt, and wanting work that isn't priced by it. What they hope for: the family's licence paid off, or a licence of their own that doesn't belong to the yard.

**In the dust towns.** A family in the dust towns past the licensed rows, on day hire, with no licence. The domes are old and patched, the dust is in the bread, and the morning queue at the yard gate decides who works. Children learn to seal a suit before they can read and to tell a good patch from a bad one by the hiss. Who is missed: a parent in the morning queue, or a grandmother who patched the dome. Why they leave: the queue. What they hope for: a licence, any licence, or the family out of the dust.

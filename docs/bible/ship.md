# Hester's ship

## The ship

A fifty-year-old Ice Hauler: a crew module of six decks stacked across the thrust, bolted to the water tank she carries, with the drive deck under the tank and a ladder well through every deck (`LAYOUTS.freighter`, `js/layouts.js`). From the nose down: the bridge; the galley and the head; the berths; the gunnery and the workshop; the airlock and the medbay; life support; the tank; the engine room and the hold. She carries contract freight that can't wait, and between contracts cuts ice at the outer Belt's rocks and sells the water where a port is short of it. She is chartered on hire-purchase from a finance house, registered at a small Belt station, and insured with that registry's mutual, which the better ports don't recognise. She has had four owners and three names.

Ownership aboard:

- The hull and drive belong to the finance house until the last payment.
- The fittings Hester has added are hers, and go with the ship if the house takes it.
- The navigation, docking and accounts software is licensed by the year. The licence lets the finance house read the accounts, so Hester also keeps her own in a paper notebook.
- The fabricator's design files are paid for by the print.
- The suits and kit are Hester's, rented to the ratings through deductions from their pay.

## The crew

**Officers**, on Hester's articles, at a day rate plus a share:

- Captain Hester Vance, who holds the charter.
- Ines Ferreira, the pilot.
- Tomas Achebe, the engineer.

**Ratings**, placed by an agency, at a lower day rate with full deductions:

- Cato Rahman, the bosun, who runs the ratings and the watch rota.
- The hired hand, the player.
- One or two deckhands.

On a run with a high-value consignment, the shipper may send a **supercargo**: their own agent, aboard as a passenger, who signs for the cargo at each end and checks it in between.

## Life aboard

**Weight.** Under cruise thrust there is a steady fraction of a g. At the flip there is none for a few minutes. Docking and departure are done strapped in.

**Money.** The charter payment falls due monthly. Fuel is the largest bill on every run, bought at depots in trade notes. Wages are paid at a port, in each person's contract currency.

**Watches.** Cato keeps the rota. A rated watchkeeper sits the bridge under cruise and calls Ines for the flip, docking and anything unexpected.

**Water and air.** Recycled and metered by the ship, rationed when the recycler struggles. Showers are on a rota.

**Food.** Mostly printed or rehydrated, with whatever was cheap at the last port.

**Messages.** The ship buys a cheap relay slot, so messages and news arrive in a batch twice a day.

**Privacy.** Berths have curtains. The galley is shared.

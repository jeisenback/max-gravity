# The Belt

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-07 (#891). Built on `economy.md`, `jurisdictions.md`, `work.md` and `shape.md`.

## What it is

Between the named places (Ceres and its co-ops, Ring Nine, the Pallas refinery, The Hollows, The Rook, Boneyard, Ironheart, Juno Commons, Eros Old Town) the Belt is full of small settlements: a family working one rock, an old mine turned into a town, hulls lashed together into a market, a ship nobody aboard has sold in thirty years. Most are off the lanes the big freighters fly. Some are owned, some govern themselves, and some answer to nobody.

The Belt is half lawless. There are pockets of order around the owners' depots and stations, freeholds that keep their own rules and refuse everyone else's, and in the gaps, pirates. It is a frontier because the owners' law is expensive to carry out here, and most of the time it does not pay to.

The game makes many of these places from the kinds below, as it makes crew from places and circumstances (#871, #885), and scatters them through the Belt, so no two runs have the same Belt.

## The kinds of settlement

| Kind | What it is | Who runs it | Law |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Depot town** | Grown up around a fuel depot or a refinery: company housing, a bar, a clinic | The owner | The owner's: company scrip, company security |
| **Hollowed rock** | An old mine whose shafts became a town when the ore ran out | A council, or whoever bought the rock | Local, written down, enforced unevenly |
| **Claim rock** | One family or a small crew working one rock under a claim | Themselves | The registry they pay for the claim, which is far away |
| **Freehold** | A settlement with no owner, run by its members in assembly; refuses outsiders' law, and trades ice and labour on its own terms | Its members | Their own; hostile to owners and lenders |
| **Raft** | Hulls lashed together into a floating market that drifts along the lanes | Nobody for long | Whoever is strongest that week |
| **Homeship** | A family or a crew living on a ship they never sell, going where the work is | The ship's people | Their own aboard, and none outside |
| **Pirate nest** | Hidden among the rocks; buys stolen cargo and sells protection | A boss, or a crew | Theirs, by force |

They fall into three rough zones of law:

- **Owned:** depot towns and the big stations. The owner's law, with the owner's security to enforce it.
- **Self-governed:** hollowed rocks, freeholds and claim rocks. They have rules, and nobody outside enforces them.
- **Nobody's:** rafts and pirate nests, out where patrols don't go.

The named places fit the kinds: Ring Nine and The Hollows read as freeholds, Boneyard as a raft that stopped moving, the Pallas refinery's tunnels as a depot town.

## What holds it together

**What every place needs:**

- **Fuel,** from the depot towns, at the owner's price. It is the Belt's leash.
- **Air and water,** which means ice. Claim rocks and freeholds mine it; depot towns and rafts buy it.
- **Parts and medicine,** from the big stations, at station prices.

**What the small places sell:** ore, ice, salvage, labour and, from Juno's growers, food.

**Where small ships fit.** Bulk goes slow and cheap on the big freighters' fixed lanes (`economy.md`). The scattered rocks are off those lanes. Small hulls carry what bulk can't reach or can't wait for: parts, medicine, mail and people out to the rocks; small loads of ore and salvage back; fuel in tanks to rocks too far to come to a depot. This is the work a co-op hull like the Ore Runner lives on.

**Who preys on whom:**

- **Pirates** take the small runs, not the escorted freighters.
- **Protection rackets** work the claim rocks and the rafts.
- **Owners** squeeze the freeholds through the price of fuel. A freehold that won't take an owner's credit pays cash or goes without.
- **Freeholds** shelter the people owners want: strikers, debtors, deserters.

## The work

A port's board in the Belt is full, and most of it is small. The game makes jobs from these kinds, each priced by distance, risk and who is paying:

- **Courier:** mail, a parcel, a sealed case, a court paper, a payroll. Small, fast, and sometimes not what it says.
- **Supply:** parts, medicine, food or fuel in tanks out to a rock off the lanes, on a deadline.
- **Haul back:** a small load of ore, ice or salvage from a rock that a freighter won't stop for.
- **Passengers,** and some of them special:
  - a doctor going out to a rock with a fever;
  - a family moving to a freehold;
  - a striker the depot's owner wants back;
  - a debtor running from a finance house;
  - a body going home to be buried;
  - a claim inspector, or a buyer, nobody wants to see;
  - someone who pays well and won't say why.
- **Tow and salvage:** a dead hull to bring in, or what can be cut off one.
- **Rescue:** a distress call, a rock whose air plant failed, a ship adrift.
- **Escort:** riding with a smaller hull through a stretch where pirates work.
- **Survey:** look at a rock for a claim, or for a buyer.
- **Smuggling:** what the owners tax or forbid, to places that need it. Fuel, in a war.

The members vote on which to take (`chapter-2.md`). The kinds make the board; the places and the people on it make each job different.

## When the war comes

The owners' war (`the-fuel-war.md`) is fought over fuel, and the Belt is where it lands. It rumbles through chapter two and breaks out in chapter three:

- Depots close or ration, and fuel prices jump. The small rocks are desperate, and running fuel to them is the best-paid and most dangerous work in the Belt, legal or not.
- Both sides hire guns. Pirates become privateers with papers.
- Freeholds are pushed to choose a side, or be starved.

The co-op's squeeze happens as it rumbles (`chapter-2.md`). Yelena, a hired gun, and Ruben, on one side's networks, come out of it.

## Assumptions to confirm

- The Belt is half lawless: owned pockets, self-governed rocks, and nobody's ground between.
- The seven kinds of settlement, and the three zones of law.
- The game generates many settlements from the kinds and scatters them through the Belt, different each run.
- Small hulls live on the work off the bulk lanes; the job kinds above are what a Belt board holds.
- Fuel is the Belt's leash and what the owners' war is fought over.
- The named Belt places fit the kinds as above.

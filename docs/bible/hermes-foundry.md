# Hermes Foundry

Status: draft for the owner's review, 2026-11-04. Built on `economy.md`, `work.md` and `jurisdictions.md`.

## The place

A refining works on Mercury's night side near the terminator, where the solar furnaces can take the Sun's full weight on their mirrors and the works can shelter from it. The furnaces smelt ore shipped in from the Belt and the works' own mines into metal for the orbital yards. The skyline is radiator fins: banks of them, kilometres long, carrying the furnaces' heat away into the dark. They glow red at the edges when the furnaces run hot.

## Who owns it

A refining company owns the works and everything in it, as one corporate space (`jurisdictions.md`): the furnaces, the quarters, the clinic, the shop, the transfer dock. Its rules are the company's, and so is its scrip. It answers to no arcology, and the code calls it independent.

## Work and pay

Almost everyone is on a two-year hazard contract, paid at hazard rates for every shift out under the Sun. The pay is good and the work is dangerous. The company houses contract workers and their families in the quarters for the length of the contract. Most mean to do one contract and save. Many renew, because a second contract is the fastest way to pay off the first one's debts. "Two more years" is what everyone says.

The radiator-fin crews are the exception. They maintain the fins, which crack in the thermal cycling and have to be welded and re-coated by hand, and they are on standing contracts the company renews without asking. They are the only people at the foundry who stay.

## Living there

The quarters are dug into the rock under the works, shielded and cool, with long corridors, a company shop, and a clinic that sees burns every day. Light is by the clock: the company runs a day and a night under the rock, and the shifts run round it. Nobody goes up to the surface without a reason. Calls home are long and expensive, and food that tastes of somewhere else is the luxury people spend on.

## Growing up here

**In the quarters, on back-to-back contracts.** A child raised in the company's quarters by parents who signed one two-year contract and then another, and another. The family always meant to leave. The children go to the company school and know the furnace shifts by the sound of the lifts. The family's money goes on calls home to wherever the parents came from, and on paying down the last contract's debt. Who is missed: a parent still on the furnace shifts. Why they leave: to stop the next two years before they start. What they hope for: to be somewhere their family chose to live.

**With the fin crews.** A child of the radiator-fin crews, the few households who stay a whole career and raise their children at the works. Fin crew children know the fins the way other children know a street, and go up with a parent on maintenance shifts from fourteen, in heavy suits, to learn the welds. The fin crews have their own corridor in the quarters and their own table in the canteen. Who is missed: a parent or grandparent still on the fins. Why they leave: the fins are a career, and they wanted to choose one. What they hope for: to come back and run a fin crew, or to see a sky that isn't the Sun's.

# Chapter 3: the war

Status: outline for the owner's review, 2026-11-14 (#1229). Built on `chapter-2.md` (its last vote), `the-fuel-war.md` (the war breaks out here), `shape.md` (the chapters widen) and `later-chapters.md` (chapter 4 opens where this one leaves the player). Nothing is built.

## What it is

The war over Vesta breaks out: Ballast's blockade first, then open fighting around Vesta and along the fuel lanes (`the-fuel-war.md`, Chapter three). Chapter 2's last vote has put the hand in one of three places, and chapter 3 is a different chapter in each. All three share the war's events, the same world and the hand's own record; what differs is where the player stands, who is with them, what is theirs, and what the war asks of them.

Each follows the funnel of `shape.md`: a short written opening that says where the player now stands, a long middle the world carries, and a decision at the end that opens chapter 4.

## What all three share

- **The war's course.** The blockade, the rationing, the small rocks going short, privateers with papers from both sides, freeholds made to choose. It runs whether the player joins it or not, and its end is the world's (`the-fuel-war.md`, How it ends); chapter 3 sees the blockade and the first fighting, and the war goes on into chapter 4.
- **The hand's record.** Who lived, who was promised what, whose side the hand took. Anyone can die (`cast.md`, Death).
- **The people of the war.** The four masters (`people/saran.md`, `kessler.md`, `mensah.md`, `belanger.md`), Yelena with Ballast and Ruben on the networks, met differently on each path.
- **Who comes with the hand.** The members follow the vote, or do not: a member who voted against the outcome and thinks poorly of the hand leaves the ship, and is met again (a thread away, `cast.md`); one who thinks well of the hand stays whatever they voted.

## The organiser's (Hold)

**Where the player stands.** The co-op kept the Ore Runner and its independence, at a price that comes from what the hand built in chapter 2 (the owner, 2026-11-14):

- **By default, the house's harder terms:** more say over cargo and lanes, payments called early.
- **A refinance, if the hand earned the standing:** a freehold's or Kaur's people take over the debt. The co-op is free of the house and owes someone in the war, which leans it toward a side without a charter.
- **A member's share sold** to pay for the hold, which costs a person and a vote.

The hand is still her captain and one of the members, and now the one the other crews come to.

**Who is with them.** The members who stayed; the hired pilot (Ines in the usual narrow run), a member or on her licence (`chapter-2.md`, the mid-chapter vote); new members recruited in the chapter, the war's recruits among the candidates (`chapter-2.md`, The cast).

**What is theirs.** The Ore Runner, the co-op's books, and a voice among the Belt's other co-ops.

**The long middle.**

- The blockade starves the small rocks, and running fuel and air to them is the co-op's best work and its most dangerous (aid, `trade-and-law.md`; air and depot fuel as goods, `trade-goods.md`).
- The lender, holding a co-op that would not charter, squeezes harder: lanes refused, cargo barred, a payment called early.
- Other co-ops' hulls are squeezed the same way. The hand can bring them together: a shared refusal of the house's terms, a common fund, escorts flown together. This is the league of co-ops of chapter 6 (`later-chapters.md`), begun.
- The war presses on the co-op to take a side anyway: Kaur's people offer protection for Belt fuel carried, Tycho's people offer a charter again on worse terms.

**How it ends.** The co-op can tow home the wreck Bexa always wanted, and make it a second hull (`later-chapters.md`, Chapter 4, the organiser's). The decision is how it is paid for: the league's common fund, a side's money, or the house's loan again, and each sets whose hulls the co-op's become.

**What it can cost.** A rock that was not supplied in time; a member lost on a fuel run; the league broken by a co-op that took a side's money.

## The master's (Charter)

**Where the player stands.** The Ore Runner is signed over to Tycho's side under charter, before the war breaks (`the-fuel-war.md`). The hand keeps the bridge, now on a real master's licence the lender approved (`progression.md`, layer 5), and answers to the charterer's orders. The members are crew on the charter's wage; the co-op holds the hull's paper and little else.

**Who is with them.** The members who stayed aboard on wages; the hired pilot, whose cheap endorsement the charter does not need (a licence of her own frees her); Ama Kessler as the house's eye on the fleet; Aron Saran on the next hull in the convoy.

**What is theirs.** A bridge and a licence, on someone else's terms. The cargo, the lanes and the fights are the charterer's.

**The long middle.**

- Convoys under Ballast escort carrying Tycho's fuel from the inner depots, past the Belt's anger.
- Orders that cross a line: stop a freehold's hauler, seize a rock's ice, fire on a ship running fuel to the starving rocks. The hand can carry them out, slow them, or refuse (the owner, 2026-11-14). Each refusal costs more: the first a mark on the licence and a fine for the ship; the second Kessler's report to the house, and the next convoy goes without the hand; the third ends the charter, and the hand is put ashore onto the outside path partway through the chapter. Noor Belanger's story is that mark.
- Tavi Mensah, Kaur's old master, offers a way across: Kaur would take a master and a hull that changed sides, and pay for it.

**How it ends.** A convoy command is offered (`later-chapters.md`, Chapter 4, the master's): the hand's own hull and two or three under orders. The decision is whose convoy: Tycho's, which the licence was earned for, or Kaur's, which it would be risked for.

**What it can cost.** A mark on the licence that no port forgets; members who leave for good over an order carried out; a freehold that remembers the hull's name.

## Outside (Sell)

**Where the player stands.** The hull was sold and the members scattered. The hand has what the co-op paid for the share and whoever came with them, and goes where the owners' rules thin out: the havens and the squats (`the-hollows.md`, `boneyard.md`, `ring-nine.md`, `triton-outpost.md`).

**Who is with them.** Those who followed, often one or two; Yelena, if she left Ballast, and Ruben, if the networks burned him; the havens' own people.

**What is theirs.** A share in a haven family's hull, or a berth on one, never a hull of their own: only owners own (`shape.md`; the owner, 2026-11-14), so the havens' families are the power the hand deals with. The money from the share, until it is spent; no paper anyone respects. A hull owned outright stays a rare goal ending, far later.

**The long middle.**

- The blockade makes the havens rich: depot fuel, arms and grey medicine run to the rocks the blockade starves, and every rock pays what it can (`trade-goods.md`, the banned goods).
- The patrols are thin and the lanes are not. Smuggling, salvage of the war's wrecks, and, for some, piracy.
- The havens' families (`later-chapters.md`, the havens' families of chapter 6) take note of a crew that keeps its word.

**How it ends.** A crew of several hulls in the havens (`later-chapters.md`, Chapter 4, outside). The decision is what kind: smugglers running fuel to the rocks, or pirates working the lanes the blockade leaves thin.

**What it can cost.** The share's money spent; a shipmate taken by a patrol; the rocks' trust, if the crew turns pirate on them.

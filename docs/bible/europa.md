# Europa

Status: draft for the owner's review (#885). Built on `economy.md`, `work.md` and `jurisdictions.md`.

## The place

An ice shell over a hidden ocean, and the pumping stations that drink from it. Europa's water fills the Belt's cisterns. The docks are a forest of long white hoses, each frozen to a glittering fringe, and beneath your feet the ice hums.

## Who owns it

The Water Authority, a company despite its name, holds the extraction rights and runs everything: the pumping stations, the docks, the habitat, the price of water at the dock. It runs it with polite, unblinking efficiency. Its staff wear a small pin with a drop of blue enamel, and touch it when they talk about the shortage at Ceres, which is good for the price.

## Staff and hose crews

The Authority's staff are salaried: engineers, hydrologists, dock controllers, administrators, with Authority housing and the pin. The hose crews connect, purge and de-ice the hoses on the docks and in the pumping stations, outside in suits for most of a shift. They're on contracts through an agency, paid by the shift, with hazard pay for the cold. Frostbite at the fingertips is how you know someone has been on the hoses for years.

## Living there

The staff live in the Authority's housing, warm and well-lit under the ice. The hose crews live in the dock habitat, by the hoses, where the hum is loudest. Both work for the same company and eat in the same canteens, at different tables.

## Growing up here

**An Authority family.** A child of the Authority's salaried staff, raised in the Authority's housing, schooled at the Authority's school, with a pin of their own at sixteen. They know to the litre what the Belt drinks and what it pays for it. Who is missed: a parent who works for the Authority. Why they leave: the Authority would hire them, and they want to know what it's like to work for someone else. What they hope for: to see the shortage at Ceres for themselves, or a post somewhere they don't have to wear the pin.

**A hose crew family.** A child of hose crews, raised in the dock habitat by the hoses, with the hum under the floor and a parent outside in a suit most of every shift. They know a frozen coupling by the sound it makes. Who is missed: a parent still on the hoses. Why they leave: the hazard pay is good, and the fingertips go. What they hope for: an Authority post with the pin and the housing, or work that isn't outside.

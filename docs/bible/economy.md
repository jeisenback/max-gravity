# The economy

## Who owns

A small class owns the things everything else depends on: the arcologies, the docks and stations, the mines and refineries, the lift off Earth, the drive yards, the fuel depots, the finance houses that lend against all of it, and the software and design libraries that every machine needs to run. Most owners are companies, and most companies are owned by other companies and by funds. A person meets the owner as a name on a bill.

Everyone else rents. A home is rented by the month with a service charge on top. Air and water are metered where they are made, which is everywhere off Earth and in the sealed tiers on it. Tools come on plans; a suit, a fabricator, a med kit are leased with the job. Software is licensed by the year. A design file is paid for each time it is printed. A licence to work is a fee renewed every few years. Medicine is a subscription, and a body with implants has payments.

Ownership is not forbidden to anyone. It is out of reach. Buying a flat outright costs more than a lifetime of rent, and the payments on the purchase are rent under another name. The few who do own small things (a bar on a station, a tug, a share of a co-op) usually owe a finance house for them.

**In everyday life:** people talk about bills, charges, plans and renewals the way we talk about the weather. A raise is measured against the service charge. A good job is one that pays the deductions and leaves something. Owning anything outright is a small, real distinction, and people notice it.

## Where the money goes

The frontier produces metal, refined fuel, ice and energy. It flows inward, to Earth's arcologies and Luna's industry, and to the companies that financed the mines and docks. What comes back out is goods, parts and the bills: the charges for air, berth, water, fuel and software that let the frontier keep working.

So the money that passes through a station mostly passes through it. The people who live there pay rent to an owner who is somewhere else. A station can be busy and poor at the same time.

**In everyday life:** a station's crowd and its shabbiness don't contradict each other. A Belt kid knows where the ore goes before they know where Earth is. People in the inner system rarely think about where their metal comes from.

## The rivalry

Owners compete for the chokepoints: the ports where cargo is weighed and taxed, the depots where reaction mass is sold, the lift off Earth, and the best mines and refineries. They compete with prices, contracts, lawyers and, when it pays, force: hired security, contract navies and incidents that are always somebody else's fault. Nobody wins for long. Ports change hands, depots are bought and closed, charters lapse.

**In everyday life:** the news is full of acquisitions, closures and incidents, and none of it reads as war. A port's owner changing means new fees and new forms. A depot closing means a longer haul and a fuel bill.

## Assumptions to confirm

- One currency, the credit, is used everywhere for convenience. It is issued by a clearing consortium of the finance houses, and the clearing fee on every payment is one of the system's quiet incomes.
- Metal and energy go inward; water, air and food are produced and rented locally. Ice is hauled within the Belt and to Mars, not to Earth.
- No owner controls more than a tenth of anything. The largest companies are big, and still one among many.

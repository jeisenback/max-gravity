# The economy

## Who owns

A small class owns the things everything else depends on: the arcologies, the docks and stations, the mines and refineries, the lift off Earth, the drive yards, the fuel depots, the finance houses that lend against all of it, and the software and design libraries every machine needs. Most owners are companies owned by other companies and funds. A person meets the owner as a name on a bill.

Everyone else rents. A home is rented by the month, with a service charge. Air and water are metered wherever they are made, which is everywhere off Earth and in the sealed tiers on it. Tools, suits and fabricators are leased, usually through the job. Software is licensed by the year. A design file is paid for each time it is printed. A licence to work is a fee renewed every few years. Medicine is a plan, paid by the person or by an employer as part of the wage, and lost with the job.

Ownership is out of reach rather than forbidden. A flat costs more than a lifetime of rent, and the payments on it are rent under another name. The few people who own something small (a bar, a tug, a share in a co-op, a ship on hire-purchase) owe a finance house for it.

## Concentrated in each place

Ownership is fragmented across the system and concentrated in each place. Lisbon's lift belongs to one company. Pallas's refinery belongs to one company. Ceres's berths are rated by one guild federation. Where there is a chokepoint, one owner holds it and charges what the traffic will bear, until a bigger owner buys it or a cheaper route opens.

## Money

Each large jurisdiction issues its own currency: arcology scrip, a corporate space's company credit, guild chits in the Belt. Wages and rents are paid in local currency.

Freight, charters, fuel contracts and anything that crosses between jurisdictions are priced in one hard trade currency, issued by the largest finance houses, which ordinary people rarely hold. Every change from local money to trade money costs a fee, and the rate is set by the houses.

A hand is paid in whatever their contract says, spends in whatever the port takes, and sends money home in the family's local currency. They lose a cut at each step and know roughly how much.

## Where things go

The frontier produces metal, ice and fuel. Refined metal goes to the orbital yards and Luna's industry, which build ships, stations and orbital power, not down into Earth's gravity well, which is short of nothing but space. Ice goes to Mars, the Belt stations and the depots, where it is split into water, air and fusion fuel. Earth sends finance, finished goods, medicine and people.

The money a station takes in mostly passes through it, to owners elsewhere. A station can be busy and poor at once.

## The rivalry

Owners compete for the chokepoints: ports, depots, lift, mines, refineries. They use prices, contracts, lawyers and, where it pays, force. Force is bought: security companies under contract, crewed by people from the same agencies and the same lower tiers as everyone else, armed by the drive yards and the arms makers who sell to all sides. A clash over a depot is an "incident" in the news and a contract dispute in court.

## Assumptions to confirm

- The trade currency's issuers are a handful of the largest finance houses, not one consortium; none can cut a jurisdiction off alone.
- The game keeps one displayed currency for play, read as the trade currency; the exchange to local money appears in text, not in the numbers.
- Ice is hauled within the Belt, to Mars and to the depots; it does not go to Earth.

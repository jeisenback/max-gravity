# The economy

## Who owns

A small class owns the things everything else depends on: the arcologies, the docks and stations, the mines and refineries, the lift off Earth, the drive yards, the fuel depots, the finance houses that lend against all of it, and the software and design libraries every machine needs. Most owners are companies owned by other companies and funds.

Everyone else rents. A home is rented by the month, with a service charge. Air and water are metered wherever they are made, which is everywhere off Earth and in the sealed tiers on it. Tools, suits and fabricators are leased, usually through the job. Software is licensed by the year. A design file is paid for each time it is printed. A licence to work is a fee renewed every few years. Medicine is a plan, paid by the person or by an employer as part of the wage, and lost with the job.

Ownership is out of reach rather than forbidden. A flat costs more than a lifetime of rent, and the payments on it are rent under another name. The few people who own something small (a bar, a tug, a share in a co-op, a ship on hire-purchase) owe a finance house for it.

## Concentrated in each place

Ownership is fragmented across the system and concentrated in each place. Lisbon's launch platform belongs to one company. Pallas's refinery belongs to one company. Ceres's berth rates are set by one guild federation. Where there is a chokepoint, one owner holds it and charges what the traffic will bear, until a bigger owner buys it or a cheaper route opens.

## Money

Each large jurisdiction issues its own currency: arcology scrip, a corporate space's company credit, guild chits in the Belt. Wages and rents are paid in local currency.

Between jurisdictions, freight, charters and fuel contracts are priced in a common unit of account: a fixed quantity of refined deuterium, the one thing every ship needs. Nobody issues the unit itself. Each large finance house issues its own notes denominated in it, and houses accept each other's notes at a small discount that changes with how far each house is trusted. Ordinary people rarely hold these notes. Every change from local money into notes, and between notes, costs a fee.

A hand is paid in whatever their contract says, spends in whatever the port takes, and sends money home in the family's local currency, losing a cut at each step.

## Fuel

A torch drive burns deuterium. Deuterium is separated from ice by enrichment, which takes large amounts of energy and plant, and the plants belong to the depot owners. This is why fuel is the largest cost of running a ship and why the unit of account is defined in it.

## Where things go

The frontier produces metal, ice and enriched fuel. Refined metal goes to the orbital yards and Luna's industry, which build ships, stations and orbital power. Earth's surface has metal of its own and pays a lot to lift anything out of its gravity well, so little goes down to it. Belt ice goes to the Belt stations and the depots, where it becomes water, air and fuel. Earth sends finance, finished goods, medicine and people.

Most of the money a station takes in leaves it, as rent, interest and fees to owners elsewhere.

## The rivalry

Owners compete for the chokepoints: ports, depots, the lift off Earth, mines and refineries. They use prices, contracts, lawyers and, where it pays, force. Force is bought: security companies under contract, crewed by people from the same agencies and the same lower tiers as everyone else, armed by the drive yards and arms makers who sell to all sides. A clash over a depot is reported as an incident and settled as a contract dispute.

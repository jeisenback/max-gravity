# Mars's domes

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-04. Built on `economy.md`, `work.md` and `jurisdictions.md`.

## The place

The domed cities of the Mariner Valley, under a butterscotch sky. The air outside still kills. Inside the domes it smells of green things and hot metal. Mars draws its water from its own polar and ground ice (`economy.md`), and has spent generations trying to make the valley breathe. Every tenth building has a mural of the day the first lake filled.

## Who owns it

Each dome is run by a dome company, and its main charge is the air bill (`jurisdictions.md`). Several domes share a patrol under a joint agreement (the code's Dome Concord). Each dome keeps its own council and its own air accounts, and the budget meetings are held in the open, with a good deal of shouting.

## The air

Every household has an air account: a share of the dome's air, billed monthly, and stencilled beside the door. The hub domes, the old and large ones near the valley's centre, bill the full air rate and keep the full share. The outer domes, smaller and newer, at the valley's edge where the farms are, bill less and ration the share in a bad season. When the share is cut, the household's door stencil is changed.

## Living there

The hub domes are cities: towers, markets, the council halls, and the lake murals. The outer domes are farms and the people who work them, under lower roofs, with grow-lights through the night and the share board in the square. People in the hub call the outer domes "the edge". People at the edge call the hub "the bill".

## Growing up here

**In a hub dome, on the full bill.** A household in a hub dome near the valley's centre, on the full air bill, with the share number stencilled beside the door. The family's arguments are the council's: the budget meetings in the square, the shouting, the vote on next year's air rate. Children go to the budget meetings from small. Who is missed: a parent who speaks at the council, or a grandfather who saw the first lake fill. Why they leave: the bill, which rises every year, and the meetings, which never end. What they hope for: the family's account paid ahead, or a seat on the council.

**At the edge, on a rationed share.** A farming household in an outer dome at the valley's edge, on a share that is cut in a bad season. The grow-lights run all night and the share board in the square says what everyone gets. Children work the rows from small and learn to breathe slowly in a rationed month. The lake murals are a promise here, not a memory. Who is missed: a mother who keeps the rows, or a sister who stayed. Why they leave: a rationed winter. What they hope for: a full share for the family, or a lake at the edge.

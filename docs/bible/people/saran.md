# Aron Saran

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-07 (#891). Built on `ceres-coop.md`, `chapter-2.md`, `work.md`, `luna.md` and `the-fuel-war.md`.

## Where he stands

The Ore Runner's master, 54, from Luna. Hired by the Ceres Crew Co-operative on a wage, and not a member: he has no share and no vote. He holds a master's licence and the bridge, as the co-op's master. In play he carries out what the player and the meeting decide (`chapter-2.md`). He can refuse an order he thinks unsafe, once, in a scene, and overruling him costs the co-op something: his trust, or a mark on the hull's papers.

His hire was one of the lender's terms. Van Leeuwen & Co. would not lend against a hull without a licensed master on her that it had approved, and it sends the co-op three names from its pool (`chapter-2.md`). Saran is one of the four masters in it; when he is chosen, this is who the co-op gets.

## Money

A master's wage, paid by the co-op before the members see anything. Out of it go:

- the rent on a flat in the Luna yards, which his wife keeps;
- his licence's renewals and the medicals that go with them, which get dearer every year after fifty;
- a debt to a Luna crewing house from the year he was without a bridge.

## Family

- **His wife, Liv**, 51, is a yard clerk at Tycho Yard Holdings on Luna (`the-fuel-war.md`). She has worked there for twenty years.
- **Their daughter, Lea**, 24, is a third officer on an inner-system freighter.

## Who he is

Upright, tidy, and careful. A good master: he flies safe, he keeps the hull's papers clean, and in a fight he is calm and quick. He keeps his opinions to the bridge, and he knows that everything he says in a meeting is in the minutes the lender reads.

He commanded freighters for Luna owners for fifteen years. His last bridge was sold out from under him and the crew, at a day's notice, to pay an owner's debt; the crew scattered and he spent a year ashore. He took the co-op's job because a members' vote is at least something he can read. An owner's mind never was.

The members are never sure whose man he is: theirs, who pays him, or the lender's, who picked him. Neither is he, some days.

He likes: a clean log; a burn on the second; his daughter's letters. He doesn't like: meetings; members who come to the bridge to argue; being thanked for doing his job.

## Aboard

- **Tomas** he respects as an engineer, and leaves the drive to him.
- **Cato** runs the ratings for him, and Saran is grateful and doesn't say so.
- **Ines** flies on an endorsement he doesn't like, and he tells her so once, on her first watch, and then never again.
- **Bexa** and **Pax** he treats as crew aboard and as owners ashore, and finds the change hard.
- **The hand** works a post under him and votes on his terms in port.

## How he talks

Formally, in short orders aboard, and carefully ashore. He calls the members by their surnames on the bridge and their first names in port.

Lines in his voice:

- "On my bridge, I decide. In your meeting, you do. I'll keep the two apart if you will."
- "I have read the lender's letter. I'll tell you what it says. I won't tell you how to vote."
- "I lost a ship once to a man I never met. I'd rather lose one to a vote."
- "Rahman, the rota. Achebe, the drive. We burn at six."
- "My wife works for Tycho. You should know that. I'd rather you heard it from me."

He doesn't say: what the lender's agent says to him privately; how he would vote, if he had one.

## What he wants

A bridge that cannot be sold out from under him, and his daughter's career clear of the debts he took on. In the last vote he is who the hull ends up answering to if the co-op charters: a charter to Tycho's side gives him a sure bridge, and his wife a safe job. He will not ask the members for it. They will wonder if he wants it.

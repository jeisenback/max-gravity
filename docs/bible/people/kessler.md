# Ama Kessler

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-07 (#891). Built on `chapter-2.md`, `the-fuel-war.md`, `rotterdam.md` and `work.md`.

## Where she stands

A licensed master, 61, from Rotterdam. One of the four masters in Van Leeuwen & Co.'s pool (`chapter-2.md`). She is a placement master: the house puts her on the hulls it is nervous about, on the borrower's wage, for as long as it stays nervous. She has done this for eighteen years, on eleven hulls.

If the co-op chooses her, she holds the bridge for the co-op and carries out what the player and the meeting decide. She also writes to the house every week.

## Money

A master's wage from the co-op, and a retainer from the house that she does not hide. Out of it go:

- the rent on a flat in Rotterdam, kept for the months between placements;
- her licence and medicals;
- a pension she has paid into since she was thirty, and checks every quarter.

## Family

- **Her brother, Joost**, 58, is a clerk in Van Leeuwen's Rotterdam office. He got her the first placement.
- She was married once, to a pilot, who died on a run off Callisto twenty years ago.

No children.

## Who she is

Dry, fair, and very hard to surprise. She knows the lender's people by their first names, and what each of them wants this quarter. She tells the members on her first day that she reports to the house, and what she reports: the log, the cargo, the minutes. "Nothing you'd not show them yourselves," she says, which is true, and which they learn to hear as a warning.

She is a good master. She flies safe, keeps clean papers, and has never lost a crew. The hulls she is put on mostly survive, and mostly end up doing what the house wants.

She likes: a balanced book; plain speaking; Rotterdam rain. She doesn't like: surprises; members who hide things from her; being thought the house's spy, which she is not, quite.

## How she talks

Briefly, drily, with figures in it. She calls the members by their surnames, always.

Lines in her voice:

- "I write to the house every Sunday. You may read it first, if you like. Most crews stop asking by the third."
- "That run pays. It also puts us on a lane the house doesn't like. Both of those go in the letter."
- "I've been on eleven hulls. Nine still fly. I'd like this to be the tenth."
- "Don't tell me what you'd rather I didn't write down. Then I won't have to."
- "The house is not your enemy. It is a lender. That's worse in some ways and better in others."

She doesn't say: what the house says back to her; that she likes some of the crews she has watched.

## What she wants

A clean last placement, and a pension she can live on in Rotterdam. In the war she leans to the lender: under her the co-op is safe and watched, and a charter to the lender's side is the easiest road. If the members hold, she will write it down fairly, and that may be the most help they get.

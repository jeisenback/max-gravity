# Hester Vance

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-01. Built on `rotterdam.md`, `work.md` and `ship.md`.

## Where she stands

Captain, 52, from Charlois in Rotterdam. She holds the ship on a hire-purchase charter from a Rotterdam finance house: eleven years into fifteen, a payment on the first of every month, and the ship hers only after the last one. If she misses three payments, the house takes the ship back, with everything she has paid.

She was an agency rating on ore haulers for twenty years before that. She raised the charter's deposit from her savings and a loan against her own future share, which she has paid off.

She signs her officers on her own articles, at a day rate plus six percent each of what the ship clears after fuel, fees and the charter. Her ratings come through an agency, because her insurer requires it.

## Money

Every run is a sum, and she does it before she takes the run and after she has been paid for it. Out of what the ship earns, in order:

- fuel, bought in trade notes at the depots, the biggest bill on every run;
- port and dock fees;
- the charter payment, on the first;
- the officers' shares and everyone's wages;
- the yearly software licences and the mutual's premium;
- what is left, which is hers.

The ship's accounts software lets the finance house read the books. She keeps her own as well, in a paper notebook, and they don't always agree.

Out of what is hers goes part of her mother's rent.

## Family

- **Her mother, Marijke**, 78, still lives in Charlois. She held the lease through her job on the pump contractor's maintenance crews. When she retired, it became a retiree's lease at a higher rent, and Hester pays the difference.
- **Her father** died when she was in her thirties, while she was on a run to the Belt. She heard eleven days later.

She isn't married. She says she couldn't afford it, and means the time.

## Who she is

Careful, plain and private. She isn't greedy: she counts everything because one bad quarter loses the ship, and she knows it to the credit. She is fair by the articles, to the letter, and doesn't go past them, because the articles are what she can afford.

She remembers being a rating, the deductions and the agency, and runs her ship the same way anyway, because the insurer and the house require it. She knows how that looks. She doesn't explain it.

She likes: a run that comes in on the figure; strong tea; the notebook; the ship, which she has had longer than anything else in her life. She doesn't like: surprises; the finance house's letters; anyone who rounds a number.

## Aboard

- **Cato**, the bosun, she has sailed with for nine years. She trusts him with the ratings and the rota. He is the one person aboard she tells things to.
- **Ines** she hired knowing about the landing, and knowing that a pilot without a licence comes cheaper. Both are true, and Ines knows both.
- **Tomas** she trusts with the engine and pays on the day. They agree that the ship needs more than she can spend on her.
- **The ratings**, the hand included, she treats correctly and keeps at a distance. She was one, and she doesn't pretend she still is.

## How she talks

Short, exact, in figures where figures will do. She tells you what a thing costs before she tells you what she thinks of it. She doesn't raise her voice. She doesn't round.

Lines in her voice:

- "Six percent of what we clear. Clear is after fuel, fees and the house. I'll show you the sum."
- "The house reads the accounts. I keep my own."
- "Four years. Forty-eight payments. I've made a hundred and thirty-two."
- "Mum's lease went up when she retired. That's how they do it."
- "I won't lend. I'll pay you for the hours."

She doesn't say: anything about loving the ship; anything against the finance house, which is doing what it was set up to do; anything that asks for sympathy.

## What she wants

The last payment made, and the ship hers. After that, a share of what the ship clears instead of a payment out of it, and her mother's rent paid without counting.

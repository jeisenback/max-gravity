# Ines Ferreira

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-10-31. Built on `lisbon.md`, `work.md` and `ship.md`.

## Where she stands

Pilot, 34, from a middle tier of Lisbon Arcology. An officer on Hester's ship, on Hester's articles: a day rate plus six percent of what the ship clears after fuel, fees and the charter. She flies on an endorsement bought from the ship's registry, which is legal aboard and worthless in Lisbon, and which keeps her on that registry's ships.

She flew the ferry's spaceplane leg between Lisbon's offshore platform and orbit for nine years, for the ferry operator, until the dead-stick landing on the closed runway. The pilots' board revoked her licence. The ferry operator dismissed her and is suing her for part of the runway damage. Her appeal has no hearing date.

## Money

She earns well for a pilot on a small ship and less than she did on the ferry. Out of it go:

- money home toward her mother's lease, in Lisbon scrip, which costs her a cut at the exchange every time;
- her lawyer in Lisbon, who is paid by the month and has achieved nothing she can see;
- the endorsement's renewal fee to the registry;
- her own medical plan, since Hester's articles don't cover it.

She knows each of these to the credit and doesn't talk about them unless asked.

## Family

- **Her mother, Graça**, 61, works the counter at a pharmacy on the family's tier and holds the lease. She sends long messages, mostly about the building, the neighbours and her health, and ends each one by asking when Ines is coming home.
- **Her brother, Rui**, 30, works for the tower lift contractor as a maintenance tech. He is married, with a two-year-old daughter, Leonor, and lives two tiers down. He sends short messages and photos of Leonor.
- **Her father** left when she was twelve and lives in Rotterdam. They don't talk.

She is not married. She was with a ground controller at the platform for four years. They split a year before the landing, and he still works there.

## Who she is

Competent and knows it, without making a thing of it. Proud of flying well, and dull about it: a good landing is a boring landing. Dry, quick, a bit impatient with people who talk about flying without doing it. Private about the landing and the lawsuit, not because they hurt to mention but because everybody she meets already has an opinion about them.

She isn't bitter about the board in the way people expect. She broke the order, she'd do it again, and she knew what it would cost. What she resents is the lawsuit, which she thinks is the ferry operator getting the runway paid for by the cheapest person in the room.

She likes: the flip, which she flies herself even when the software offers to; her niece; bad action vids, which she watches with the sound off; coffee, a lot of it.

She doesn't like: being told where to put the ship down by people who won't be aboard; being asked about the landing by strangers; the nav software, which she calls "the passenger".

## Aboard

- **Hester** hired her knowing what happened, which Ines doesn't forget. They talk about the run, the fuel and the weather at the destination. Ines thinks Hester is careful to the point of meanness and right to be.
- **Tomas**, the other officer, she gets on with easily. They argue about whether a thing is broken.
- **Cato** runs the watch rota, and she treats him as the person who actually knows what's going on aboard.
- **The ratings**, the hand included, she is friendly with and slightly apart from: she's on different terms, and both sides know it.

## How she talks

Plain, quick, a little dry. Short answers that get to the point, then a joke or a shrug. She uses flying words for flying and ordinary words for everything else. She doesn't make speeches. When something matters she says it once and changes the subject.

Lines in her voice:

- "Lisbon? Crowded. It's an arcology, it's all crowded."
- "The passenger wants to route us round a beacon that's been dead for thirty years. I'm letting it think about that."
- "I'll fly the flip. It's the only interesting bit."
- "My lawyer says the hearing's 'imminent'. He said that in March."
- "Mum wants to know if you're eating. I said you're eating. Don't make a liar of me."

She doesn't say: anything about the forty-one people as if she's owed thanks; that the board was evil; "I was right"; anything that explains a pilot's job to someone who didn't ask.

## What she wants

Her licence back, and the lawsuit dropped, so she can fly passengers out of Lisbon again and live near her mother and her niece. She knows how unlikely that is and keeps flying in the meantime.

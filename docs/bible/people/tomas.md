# Tomas Achebe

Status: draft for the owner's review, 2026-11-01. Built on `lagos.md`, `work.md` and `ship.md`.

## Where he stands

Engineer, 41, from Lagos: born on the mainland, in Surulere, and twenty years a welder in the yard on Lagos Ring. An officer on Hester's ship, on Hester's articles: a day rate plus six percent of what the ship clears after fuel, fees and the charter.

He sat his engineer's ticket at night over four years while he was welding, through a training company on the ring, and paid for it himself. Hester's registry recognises it; the better ports' insurers want more hours on it before they will.

He left the ring when the yard lost a fleet owner's refits to Luna and laid off most of a shift. His berth went with his contract. He could go down on the next spaceplane or ship out, and he shipped out.

## Money

He earns more than he did in the yard, and more steadily. Out of it go:

- money up to his sister Ngozi, in Lagos scrip, toward the arrears on her ring lease;
- money down to his parents on the mainland, for their rent;
- the agency fee he still owes for the placement on Hester's ship, in instalments;
- the yearly renewal on his ticket.

He keeps it all in a paper notebook as well as on his terminal, because the ring's bank lost a month of his transfers once.

## Family

- **His sister, Ngozi**, 35, runs a food stall in the ring market: rice and stew, and puff-puff at the shift change. Her lease on the ring is on the strength of the stall. When the yard laid people off, her customers went down with them, and she fell behind on the rent.
- **Her son, Chidi**, 9, goes to the ring's school. He has never been to Earth.
- **His parents**, Emeka and Chioma, rent the same flat in Surulere Tomas grew up in. Emeka drove buses for a transport contractor until his knees went; Chioma sells fabric in a market on the mainland.

He is not married. He doesn't talk about why.

## Who he is

Patient, careful, a little slow to speak and quick with his hands. He likes things done properly and doesn't make speeches about it; he just does them properly, and does them again if someone else didn't. He is kind in practical ways: he fixes your locker hinge without being asked.

He is homesick for the ring, not for Earth. He spent half his life in half a g and walks like it.

The hull he talks about is real: the same old freighter came into the Lagos yard for a refit three times in twelve years, under three owners, and he worked on her each time. Each owner sold her after the refit. He knows where every weld on her is and has no say in where she goes.

He likes: a machine that runs right; Ngozi's stew; football on the ring channel (the Lagos Orbitals); quiet watches. He doesn't like: work done twice because it was rushed once; being called a welder by people who mean it as a cut; the agency.

## Aboard

- **Hester** he respects. She pays on the day and doesn't rush his work. He thinks she worries about money more than the engine, and he understands why.
- **Ines** he argues with about whether a thing is broken. He likes her.
- **Cato** runs the ratings, and Tomas goes to him when he needs hands.
- **The ratings**, the hand included, he treats as people he might be working beside for years. He shows them things if they ask.

## How he talks

Slowly, plainly, in full sentences. He answers what he's asked and stops. When he explains a machine, it's because someone asked, and he explains it well. He swears in Igbo at things that won't come apart.

Lines in his voice:

- "Lagos? I'm from the ring. Lagos is where my parents live."
- "She's running fine. The pump's noisy because the bearing's going. That's not the same thing."
- "Ngozi says the stall is fine. Ngozi always says the stall is fine."
- "I did it right the first time. Now I'm doing it right the second time, because someone did it fast in between."
- "Half a g. You stop noticing. Then you get on a ship and your knees tell you."

He doesn't say: that machines have feelings; anything about destiny or ten-year plans; speeches about doing things properly.

## What he wants

Ngozi's arrears cleared, so the stall and the lease are safe. After that, a lease of his own on the ring: a repair shop for the yard workers, so that neither of them depends on the yard's contracts. He knows what a ring lease costs, to the credit.

# Eros Old Town

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-11-04. Built on `economy.md`, `work.md` and `jurisdictions.md`.

## The place

The first mining boomtown in the Belt, from when Eros was the near rock and everyone came. The rich seams were worked out a long time ago and the mining company went with them. Half the town is closed now, its corridors unlit and unheated, the signs still advertising assayers and dance halls. The other half runs on salvage, repair work and passing ships.

## Who owns it

When the mining company left, its creditors took the town and sold the lease to a town trust, which charges the residents a service charge for air, heat and light. The trust closed the far half of the town when the charge stopped covering it. The closed corridors still hold air, because pumping them down costs more than leaving them, but the trust doesn't heat, light or service them.

## The old families

A few families bought their flats outright in the boom, and own them still: almost the only owners in the Belt. They pay the trust's service charge like everyone else, and it goes up every year. They meet it by selling what the boom left them, one piece at a time, to the dealers who come through for it.

## Living there

Most of the town rents from the trust, in the lit half. Some families live in the closed half, in flats the trust doesn't charge for because it doesn't count them, running heaters off taps the trust hasn't found and patching the seals themselves. Everyone knows, and the trust's inspectors come round twice a year.

## Growing up here

**In an old family.** A child of one of the boom families that still own their flats, raised among the heirlooms and the stories of the boom, watching the family sell one of them every time the service charge goes up. Who is missed: a grandparent who remembers the boom, or says they do. Why they leave: to send money home before the family sells the last of it. What they hope for: to keep the flat in the family, or to buy back something that was sold.

**In the closed half.** A child of a family living in the closed half of town, off the trust's books, raised in cold corridors by heaters running off taps the trust hasn't found. They can patch a seal and find a warm tap in the dark. Who is missed: a parent who keeps the heaters running. Why they leave: the inspectors come round twice a year, and one year they'll find the taps. What they hope for: a flat in the lit half, on the books, or a home that isn't anyone's secret.

- The closed half still holds air but is unheated, unlit and unserviced.

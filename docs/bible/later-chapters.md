# The later chapters

Status: draft for the owner's review, 2026-11-08. Built on `shape.md`, `chapter-2.md`, `the-fuel-war.md`, `the-belt.md`, `work.md` and `trade-and-law.md`. Chapters 4 to 6 were agreed in conversation; chapter 7's threat is not settled.

## What this is

`shape.md` maps the game to chapter 3 and says the chapters widen until the world is the game. This page draws the chapters after that, so that fleets, outposts and alliances each have a place to arrive, and so that what chapter 2 builds (standing, the co-op's books, the board's kinds of work) is built with them in mind. Nothing here is new machinery: the parked milestones of `ROADMAP.md` (the shipping company, the frontier and legacy, the faction states) come back one chapter at a time, under the world of owners rather than the old factions.

Each later chapter keeps the funnel of `shape.md`: a short written start that says where the player now stands, a long stretch the world carries, and a decision that sets where the next chapter starts. The spine thins with each one. By chapter 6 the written start is a page and the decision is the player's own.

## How the later chapters play

Each chapter stays its own slice, a little wider than the one before. What *King of Dragon Pass* lends is how a wider slice plays, not a new structure:

- **The ring.** Decisions are argued by the people around the player, and the ring grows with the chapters: the crew, the co-op's members, a fleet's masters, a bloc's delegates. Their sheets and opinions are the ones the game already keeps.
- **The calendar and the pool.** Time passes in the same days and burns; events come from one pool, drawn by what the player holds and the state of the world.
- **Holdings.** What *King of Dragon Pass* does not have: hulls to crew and refit, outposts to supply. Each chapter adds one kind, and what a path keeps stays in the later chapters, so on the organiser's path a run may hold two hulls and a rock by chapter 6. A path that sells or scatters keeps less: chapter 2's sell vote leaves no co-op hull.
- **A coarser step.** A day at a time does not carry two hulls and an outpost. The later chapters pass time in larger steps (a burn, a month, a season), and the player can still drop into a day aboard or ashore when a scene wants it. This is an assumption to test before chapter 4 is built.
- **Succession.** When the hand dies or retires, the run goes on with someone from the ring, at a cost: the holdings kept, standing and regard cut, as `js/legacy.js` already does for the parked owner game.
- **The levers.** A fleet or a place puts the player where the owners and agencies stood in chapter 1: hiring ratings, setting deductions, signing charters. The endings read what the player did with them.

## The chapters

## Chapter 4: more hulls

The chapter opens where chapter 3's path left the player, and gives them a second hull.

- **The organiser's.** The co-op tows Bexa's wreck home (`chapter-2.md`: the ship everybody else gave up on) and refits it at Ceres. A hired master runs it on routes of her own, on the co-op's books; the members vote on which. This is the shipping company milestone (`ROADMAP.md`, 2: hired captains running routes, escorts that fly with you) brought back as co-op hulls. The crews are hired as `work.md` says crews are: agency ratings, licensed officers, a master the lender must approve.
- **The master's.** A convoy command for Tycho's side or Kaur's (`the-fuel-war.md`): the master's own hull and two or three under orders, carrying fuel through the blockade. The licence that chapter 3 earned is what makes it possible, and a mark on it is what the chapter can cost.
- **Outside.** A crew of several hulls in the havens (`the-hollows.md`, `boneyard.md`, `ring-nine.md`): smugglers running fuel to the rocks the blockade starves, or pirates working the lanes it leaves thin.

**Fleet combat** arrives here, as an option and not the only fight: a convoy on the sensor board (`js/board.js`), each hull a return with a post and a person at it, the player's orders to each in the beats. It is the raid in beats with more than one ship on each side, not a new kind of combat (`COMBAT.md`). The exploration and salvage steps (`ROADMAP.md`, 7) feed it: a hull is found before it is towed, and the parts cut off wrecks fit the hulls.

The decision: which hull the player stands on, and so who they are to the people on the other.

## Chapter 5: a place

An outpost. The player's side of the war, or their flight from it, lands them on a rock: founding a claim rock, joining a freehold, or taking over a hollowed rock (the kinds of `the-belt.md`). It is supplied from the hold, with buildings and settler moments, as the frontier milestone built it (`ROADMAP.md`, 5: Callisto or Nereid), moved into the Belt and made of the Belt's kinds of place.

- **What it makes.** Ice, and so air, water and fuel stock; ore; a yard's work if it has a yard. This is the economy borrowing (`ROADMAP.md`, 7): the player's own industry rather than trade in others' markets, sold into a war that wants fuel above all. A rock that makes fuel in a fuel war is a prize, and the war notices it.
- **Who comes.** Settlers from the places the war has broken: the Warren's renters (`ceres-coop.md`), tug hands with no wrecks (`boneyard.md`), strikers and debtors the freeholds shelter (`the-belt.md`). Each with a family line and a story by place, as the crew have (`docs/bible/people`).
- **Whose law.** A claim rock pays a far registry; a freehold refuses everyone's law; a hollowed rock has a council. The player's choice of kind is a choice of who can come for them (`jurisdictions.md`, `trade-and-law.md`).

The decision: whether the place is the player's own, or the bloc's.

## Chapter 6: alliances

The war is decided by blocs, and the player's place is in one. The blocs grow out of what the Belt already has (`work.md`: "they organise where they can"):

- **a league of co-ops**, the organiser's path grown up: hulls that bargain with the lenders together and refuse a charter together;
- **the freeholds' assembly**, which has refused owners' law since before the war and now has to hold fuel and air together or lose both;
- **an owner's charter**, Tycho's or Kaur's, for a master who has made their career inside it;
- **the havens' market families** (`the-rook.md`), who buy what the war spills and sell protection from it.

Standing, which `trade-and-law.md` makes the currency of the Belt's work and chapter 2 starts to keep, is what is spent here: for a vote in the league, a seat in the assembly, a contract from the owner, a word with the families. The faction states of the old game (`ROADMAP.md`, 1: economies that boom and slump, incidents that build to war) come back as the blocs' states.

The decision: the war's end, tipped by the player's bloc. `the-fuel-war.md` names the three ends; the player does not decide which alone, but by chapter 6 the bloc they built is one of the hands on the scale.

The goal endings of `shape.md` land in this chapter for most runs: a fleet organised, a master's licence, a life made on the edge, rich and ignoring every thread.

## Chapter 7: the threat

A thing from outside the Belt's politics, which every bloc of chapter 6 has to answer, together or not. It is a finale: what the player built is put to the test, and the run ends on what held. The rule of `shape.md` stands: no interstellar war, no great power, this one system. So the threat is from outside the Belt, not outside the system.

**The threat is not settled.** Three fit the rule, and the page names them so the choice is made against the world and not in the code:

1. **The arcologies act together.** Earth's holding companies (`README.md`: the arcologies replaced the nations) have stayed home through the whole war, with the lift and the largest yards. They come to end it on their terms: a combined fleet, a settlement imposed, a levy on everything that moves. The one power the Belt has always feared, and the first time it moves as one. Every bloc decides whether to bend, bargain or fight, and standing is what is spent. Uses everything built; changes nothing in the bible.
2. **The sky.** A long-period comet breaking up through the Belt, or a solar maximum that burns relays and strands ships for weeks. No enemy, only a thing the blocs answer together or do not. Strong on the outposts' survival side, weaker as a finale, since nobody chose it.
3. **Something returns.** A ship that left generations ago on a slow drive comes back, changed. Human, so the rule is only bent, but a new kind of thing in the world, and it would need pages of its own before anything else.

The decision is the run's last: the ending reads the record of all seven chapters (`shape.md`, Endings).

## What chapter 2 should keep in mind

So that these chapters can be built without reopening chapter 2:

- **Standing** is kept by name of the body that holds it (a shipper, a lender, a freehold, a bloc), not only by port, so a bloc in chapter 6 can read it.
- **A hull is a record** with a name, a registry, a master and a crew, so that a second hull in chapter 4 is another of the same and not a special case of the player's.
- **A site is a place** (`ROADMAP.md`, 7, step 1): a rock the outpost of chapter 5 can be founded on is a site that stays.
- **The war's state** is the world's, read by every chapter, as `the-fuel-war.md` has it.

- Fleet combat arrives in chapter 4 as a convoy on the sensor board, an option beside the single-hull raid, and not a new kind of combat.
- The outpost of chapter 5 is of the Belt's kinds of place, and its industry is the economy borrowing.
- The blocs of chapter 6 are the league of co-ops, the freeholds' assembly, an owner's charter and the havens' families; standing is what is spent in them.
- Chapter 7 is a threat from outside the Belt's politics and inside the system; which one is open, with the arcologies acting together as the written case.
- The parked milestones come back under this map and are not rebuilt from nothing.
- The later chapters play as *King of Dragon Pass* does: a growing ring, one pool, holdings a path keeps.
- Time passes in a coarser step than a day from chapter 4, with a drop into a day; to be tested.
- From chapter 4 a death or a retirement passes the run to someone from the ring, at a cost.

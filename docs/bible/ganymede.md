# Ganymede

Status: draft for the owner's review (#885). Built on `economy.md`, `work.md` and `jurisdictions.md`.

## The place

A moon of leasehold terraces at Jupiter: mirror arrays turning slowly overhead, feeding sunlight to agri-domes whose crops feed half the Belt. From the dock it looks like a small green sea under glass, with crop rows running to the horizon and irrigation booms tracing their slow arcs.

## Who owns it

A land company owns the moon, the mirrors and the domes, and leases the terraces to farming families. A lease runs for the holder's life and passes to one child, with a renewal fee the company sets each time it passes. The company also sets the price of mirror time, which every terrace needs. The farmers are practical, sunburned, and wary of anyone who talks like a lease agent.

## Leaseholders and hands

A leasehold family farms its own terrace and sells the crop to the company's buyers. It can't sell the lease, only pass it on. The terraces need more hands than the families have at planting and harvest, so the leaseholders hire seasonal hands, who come up on the haulers from the Belt, are paid by the harvest, and live in bunkhouses at the terrace edge.

## Living there

The leasehold families live on their terraces, in farmhouses under the domes, and have done for generations. The hands' families live in the bunkhouses or follow the seasons round the moon from terrace to terrace. Everyone eats well. The leaseholders watch how you pay.

## Growing up here

**On a lease.** A child of a leasehold family, raised in the farmhouse on the family's terrace, working the rows from the age they could walk them. They know the price of mirror time and what the renewal fee was when the lease last passed. Who is missed: a parent who holds the lease. Why they leave: the lease passes to one child, and the renewal fee takes everything the family saved. What they hope for: a lease of their own, or enough sent home that the next renewal doesn't take the farm's savings.

**A hands' family.** A child of seasonal hands, raised in bunkhouses at the terrace edges, moving round the moon with the planting and the harvest, paid by the crop. They've worked more terraces than most leaseholders have seen. Who is missed: a parent still following the harvest. Why they leave: a bad harvest pays nothing, and the bunkhouses are for the season, not for living. What they hope for: a lease, which hands almost never get, or a job that pays by the month.

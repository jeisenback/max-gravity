## 1. A living solar system
- **Orbits and launch windows** (started): every location orbits at its real period, so travel time and reaction mass change over the months. The system map shows the best upcoming window for a plotted burn.
- **Pirate unrest and NPC shipping** (done): raids flare up, NPC haulers avoid dangerous lanes, and shortages and gluts follow; killing pirates clears the lanes.
- **Faction states** (done): economies boom and slump; incidents build tension until two factions go to war, with navies fighting in each other's space and wartime demand in their markets.
- **Markets with real stockpiles** (done): a station runs short because nobody hauled the goods, and NPC traders actually carry cargo.
- The Cold Water story becomes one case of this general system.

## 4. Expanse-grade combat, on the console
The captain decides and the crew flies: piloting and real-time fighting are the least interesting part of the game. Design: `COMBAT.md`.
- **4a**, in the old flight model: **torpedoes and point-defense turrets** (done), escorts (done in milestone 2), **boarding and capture, and a medic who matters** (done).
- **4b**, momentum flight and real-time burn fights: built as a prototype, now **dropped**. Retired in favor of the card duel (#52). `js/engage.js` stays, but only as the contact trigger during burns: it raises the contact and hands it to the duel (`js/duel.js`).
- **4c**, card combat: a threat-and-answer ship duel with decks built from the ship (#42), made the only combat (#52 in burns, #55 in local space and #56 the story set pieces, all done), with automated flight and the flight screen as a backdrop (#53), a boarding duel (#43), cards from every system and post (#47), and a balance simulation (#45). Hardcore mode as an option (#44) is not planned; that people can be lost is a rule of the normal game instead (#357). For the hired hand the fight is the raid in beats (section 7), not the duel; the next chapters' design puts the raid on a sensor board (#680) and folds the duel into it after.

## 7. The crew and the ship as a place
The game leans toward role-playing: people, what they think of you, and a ship you live in. Built so far: the two-deck cutaway and the Interior deck plan drawn from it, a page for each crew member (what they have told you, their bonds, their mood), portrait cards, your own page, and faces in scenes. Next, in rough order:
- **Consequence notes after a choice** (done, #281): the result screen says what the choice did to how people feel ("Ines thinks better of you", "Mara and Ines are further apart"). It reports after the fact, since most outcomes are not known in advance.
- **Click a person in the burn view** (done, #323; `js/game.js` finds the person under a tap in the cutaway, `js/character.js` opens their page): a tap on a person comes before the room under them, and does nothing while a scene is open (`tests/burnview.test.js`).
- **A chapter recap at the buy-in** (done: "Looking Back", #275): runs and earnings, who you got close to, marks and memories, and favours done or left undone, from what the game already keeps.
- **A ship that has a night** (done in the cutaway, `js/shiplife.js`): every 40 seconds of a burn the lights go down for a night, the pilot and engineer keep the watch, everyone else turns in, and the rooms hold different people at different hours. Scenes and events do not read it yet.
- **Skill-gated choices for the hired hand** (done): a choice can need your post and your level there (`post`, `skill` on a choice). One for another post is hidden, and one you have not the level for is shown shut. Used so far in the needling in the galley and the cast scenes (Ines, Tomas, Yelena, Ruben, Pax) at level 2, the captain's hot-drive order (pilot and engineer, level 2), the lane scene (one choice for each post, level 2), and the captain's praise (a bonus with the figures, level 3). More scenes can use it as they are written.
- **Stakes for the hired hand** (started): a captain whose opinion of you falls to -2 warns you once, and at -3 at a later port puts you ashore, so the chapter starts again with another captain (you keep your savings and post experience, `js/stakes.js`). A feud that has been seen and has dropped to -5 splits the crew: one of them gets off at the next port. The injury to you that sets your post level down for some days is built (`hurtHand`, `js/boarders.js`). A real cost in credits for a bad gamble at your post was weighed and left out (2026-10-16): a lost bold call already costs position, hull (which comes out of the run's profit before your share), a 15 to 35 percent chance of being hurt, the captain's opinion (up to -2 with Hester, whose nerve is 1) and experience, and the berth is at stake. Over seeds 11 to 40 with random answers, 5 of 30 chapters ended with the hand put ashore, and a chapter lost 3.3 bold calls on average (all kinds, 99 in 30 chapters). A money cost would punish the same call twice. Revisit if players do not feel the stakes: the options were docking half the run's share on a lost bold call, or having the hand pay a quarter of the bill for hull lost on their own call. **Jobs ashore, measured** (2026-10-16, `node tools/soak.js --jobs`, seeds 11 to 30, random answers, a station job at every landing with a board and every job elsewhere put to the captain): a chapter works 13 station jobs for 1,320 cr and 3.5 jobs elsewhere for 1,670 cr, 21 percent of the credits in hand at the end, and the jobs hurt the hand 3.5 times a chapter against 0.5 without them. That is the chapter's main danger: with jobs 14 of 20 chapters reached the ship's price (4 died on a job's hurt, 3 were put ashore after lost calls marked them), against 19 of 20 without, and the ones that reached it took 112 days against 98. Decided with contracts (2026-10-17, step 4): a hurt on a job ashore is the clinic and the days, never the death roll, and the job board is the day's; a lost check on another post's job still marks the captain's regard. **The bar, measured** the same way (one patron and one choice at random each landing, the soak's bar report): a chapter makes 17 choices at a table and has 3 scenes with a main character; the talks cost 110 cr, teach 0 points, raise opinion by 9 over everyone known and bring 3.5 rumors (which, since contracts, move the freight board: word of a shortage at a port is why contracts to it pay more). A fifth of the choices are leaving them to their drink.
- **Combat stakes for the hired hand** (started): a pirate contact now plays as an authored raid in beats (`js/engagements.js`), not the card duel: the closing and two passes, each a choice of how to meet her or the job of your own post, then she breaks off, stands off or comes alongside. Corsairs fight with torpedoes and raiders with grapples, and the beats read differently for each. when a boarding run lands in the ship duel, a hired hand fights the boarders at the lock, the corridor and the bridge (`js/boarders.js`): hold, rush or go round them, or do the job of your own post. Casualties come with it: a hurt hand works a level lower for some days, a crew member hurt twice in one fight is dead (a main character is marked instead), and a lost bridge costs a third of the ship's fund. The navy stop, the distress call and boarding a crippled raider are built (the contacts bullet below). - **The captain plans on what she knows** (tried 2026-10-17 and removed the same day by contracts, below; the figures stay as the record of why): the far end of a run was priced by a current rumor (which the hand may have heard at the bar), else by the prices she saw there when she last sailed from it, else by what that port usually pays; the run panel says which. The sale there is at the real price, so a run can clear less than she planned, or lose money. She sails for `WORTH_THE_FUEL` (2,000 cr a day to the ship, `js/stay.js`), and waits for the market below it, never at the port she signed the hand on at. Measured 2026-10-17 (seeds 11 to 20, forty legs, the soak's runs and nights lines): before, 0 nights and 98 days to the ship's price; with her old omniscience and the wait at 2,000, 6.9 nights and 112 days, runs planned at 4,004 cr a day and cleared at 2,019 (her own selling pushes the price down as she sells, which the planner has always ignored), 8 percent losing money; planning on what she knows, 9.3 nights and 126 days, planned 3,687 and cleared 1,691, 16 percent losing money (at 1,500 a day: 4.5 nights, 115 days). Contracts answered it: with the pay on the paper before she sails there is nothing to know, and the rumors keep their use through the board's premiums.
- **Contracts** (step 1 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-17-contracts-design.md`, built 2026-10-17, `js/contracts.js`): the captain carries freight for a named shipper. A trading port draws a board each day (none, one or two contracts, from what it ships to a port in reach that prices it higher, with dice of the board's own so no other draw in the game moves), the captain takes the one that pays the ship most a day that her hold can fill (a contract to a yard, or light to the nearest yard, once the hand has the money), the cargo is the shipper's and costs nothing, and the shipper pays on delivery, docked a tenth a day late and never below half; short of the load there is no pay. Speculation and the planner's knowledge of prices (`js/known.js`) are gone. Measured at the first numbers (rate 20 cr a ton a day, fee 1,000 a contract, seeds 11 to 20): 10 of 10 reached the ship's price in 155 days, 12 runs and 14.7 nights in port a chapter, runs planned at 2,300 cr a day and cleared at 2,185, none losing money. Steps 2 to 5 (2026-10-17): the shipper pays for what arrives, in proportion, and the ledger says what was short or late; a morning whose board has a contract worth the fuel ends the wait, and the soak counts the boards, the contracts offered, delivered, late and lost; the job board is the day's, and a hurt on a job ashore is the clinic and the days, never the death roll. Tuned (seeds 11 to 20, forty legs): a long haul pays less a day than a short one (the rate falls two percent a day of the burn, to half) and near lanes come up more, which brought a chapter of four twenty-five-day runs back to sixteen of a week; the captain sails for 1,000 cr a day to the ship (2,000 made her wait on half the boards); the rate is 26 cr a ton a day and the fee 1,200 a contract. Result: 10 of 10 chapters reach the ship's price in 120 days (98 before contracts, by the owner's choice longer now), 16 runs and 7 nights in port a chapter, runs cleared at 2,282 cr a day, none late or lost, 17 of 164 runs light. Step 6, the shippers as people, is not started.
